# LEAD: TWO GATES CLOSED, ONE OPENED

KICKER: Texas closed the grid door and narrowed the behind-the-meter exemption in the same week a 1.5 GW islanded campus was announced in Reeves County.

The week produced three developments that bear directly on the physical-layer thesis, and they do not all point the same way.

First, at the August 20 open meeting the PUCT granted all three ERCOT good-cause exceptions under Project 59142, permitting conditional Batch 0 classification by August 31 in place of the missed August 7 deadline, permitting conditionally classified base loads into the August 1 and November 1 QSA, and permitting a 24-day cure window for dynamic-data deficiencies. ERCOT separately informed the Commission that it has paused approvals to energize large-load data-center and crypto facilities until Batch 0 eligibility verification is complete. The verification sequence runs RFIs August through September, additional RFIs and cure October through November, and a Batch 0 eligibility report on December 10.

Second, and more consequential for the thesis: in the Project 58481 discussion at that same meeting, the requirements were stated to apply to all loads at or above 75 MW regardless of load type, regardless of co-location, and regardless of whether the load sits behind the meter or in front of it. This report has carried behind-the-meter and off-grid configurations as a regulatory moat since the Series A Vol 21 cut on August 7. As a categorical claim, that is now falsified. What survives is narrower and still valuable: BTM avoids the interconnection queue, the study cycle, and the energization pause. It does not avoid the disclosure, verification, audit, penalty, and financial-security regime that 58481 will impose.

Third, on August 20 Prometheus Hyperscale and Istmo Energy announced a 1.5 GW campus in Reeves County with a development path to 2.5 GW, islanded from ERCOT, initially fueled by pipeline gas, adding ethane from an adjacent Istmo fractionation facility expected in service Q1 2028, closed-loop cooled, first power targeted 2027. No customer has been named.

<<<ANGLE
The physical-versus-financed test resolves the same way it has all year, but the mechanism has shifted. Texas has not made West Texas land less valuable. It has made the qualifying attributes of that land more specific and more expensive to demonstrate. Every incremental requirement in 58481 (site control evidence, financial security at $50,000/MW payable at IA execution, data verification with penalties for inaccuracy, transmission-capacity reallocation on a 24-month milestone miss) transfers value from applicants holding queue positions to holders of documented, permitted, water-attached, gas-adjacent acreage. The energization pause prices that transfer immediately: a financed claim on a project that cannot be energized on a known date is worth less this week than it was last week, while the acre, the acre-foot, and the 2031 turbine slot did not move.

# IV. TEXAS REGULATORY: THE 58481 APPLICABILITY RULING

KICKER: 75 MW and above, co-located or standalone, behind the meter or in front. That sentence is the week's most important regulatory fact.

Project 58481 detail as stated at the August 20 open meeting:

| Element | Stated position |
|---|---|
| Applicability | All loads at or above 75 MW, regardless of load type, co-location status, or BTM versus front-of-meter |
| Financial requirement | $50,000/MW, payable at Interconnection Agreement execution |
| IA execution window | 60 days after completion of the batch study |
| Batch 0 refundability, base loads | Depends on the eligibility criteria satisfied (development readiness, contractual commitments) |
| Batch 0 refundability, studied loads | Approx. 80% refundable, 20% non-refundable |
| Future batches, 0 MW allocated and no IA | 100% refundable |
| Future batches, MW allocated and no IA | 80% refundable, 20% non-refundable |
| Direct interconnection costs | Invoiced by utilities as incurred |
| Data verification | Audit and verification provisions under development, with penalties for inaccurate or false information |
| Capacity reallocation | ERCOT may reallocate transmission capacity on a 24-month milestone miss |

Project 59142 outcome: all three ERCOT good-cause exceptions granted. Conditional Batch 0 classification by August 31; conditionally classified base loads eligible for the August 1 and November 1 QSA; 24-day cure period for dynamic-data deficiencies notified after August 7.

Verification sequence: RFI issuance and information gathering August to September; additional RFIs and cure October to November; Batch 0 eligibility report December 10. ERCOT stated at the August 14 open meeting that it expects verification to take several months but less than nine. Six projects were identified at that meeting as subject to specified treatment; remaining GCE requests (financial-security relief, Form W deadline relief, or both) will be addressed in a later filing. A separate community-impact information request will reach data centers and crypto facilities at 25 MW and above. Batch Zero study deadline of April 9, 2027 is retained.

This cut restates the fee stack correction of record. Per 16 TAC section 25.194 and the 58481 discussion: $50,000/MW financial requirement, plus study fees, plus staged security. The $100,000 plus $100,000 construction carried in Series A Vols 19 and 20 is superseded and should not be cited.

<<<ANGLE
Two thresholds now exist and they do different work. 75 MW triggers the interconnection and financial-security regime. 25 MW triggers the community-impact disclosure. A developer sizing at 74 MW per legal entity is the obvious arbitrage and the audit-and-penalty provisions under development are the obvious counter. The 24-month reallocation rule is the most underappreciated line: it converts a queue position from an option into a use-it-or-lose-it lease on transmission capacity, which is precisely how a scarce physical right should be structured and precisely what destroys the value of speculative queue inventory.

# V. TRANSMISSION: THE 765 kV BUILD IS NOW A POLITICAL QUESTION

KICKER: No final decision August 21. Two segments consolidated on need. Two commissioners absent. Back on the agenda August 28.

On August 21 the PUCT took up Docket 59029 (Oncor, Longshore Switch to Drill Hole Switch, approx. 160 miles, 10 West Texas counties including Reeves, Loving, Culberson, Winkler) and Docket 59315 (Oncor, Dinosaur Switch to Longshore Switch, approx. 235 to 270 miles, 22 counties). Both this line and the southern Howard to Solstice line are on the August 28 agenda.

Contested record in the window:

- SOAH ALJs Pratibha Shenoy and Brent McCabe found Oncor out of compliance with the pre-filing public-meeting requirement, having added route links after the June 2025 meetings that affected approximately 1,650 additional property owners who received no pre-filing meeting notice. The ALJs cited Public Utility Commission of Texas v. City of Denton (Texas Fifteenth Court of Appeals, 2026) for the proposition that an agency may not disregard its own rules.
- Oncor counsel Jaren Taylor argued HB 5066 requires the Commission to develop a plan to implement each reliability plan and ensure timely development and approval of necessary transmission improvements, and that the statute is not a study bill. Gleeson has testified to both chambers that HB 5066 did not explicitly mandate 765 kV; HB 5066's author, Rep. Charlie Geren, has disputed that his law authorized a 765 kV buildout.
- ALJs in the Bell County East to Big Hill docket cited intervenor evidence of new and planned Permian gas generation that could reduce or eliminate the need for the proposed path, and found that Oncor and LCRA TSC did not prove sufficient generation would be available to send power westward. Judges recommended rejection (reported August 21).
- Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick and Sen. Charles Schwertner called for denial of all pending applications pending legislative reform. On August 21, 31 members of the Texas House filed a letter to the same effect. Gov. Abbott has called for guidelines and guardrails on transmission expansion. All five commissioners are Abbott appointees.
- Texas Public Policy Foundation estimates lifetime cost of all STEP projects ERCOT approved through December 2025 at approximately $100 billion, borne by ratepayers.

<<<ANGLE

The transmission denial case and the powered-land case are the same case.
