# LEAD - August Opened With Two Regime Changes, and Only One of Them Was Priced

The first regime change was the one everyone watched: peace got priced into crude. After the tenth consecutive strike night that closed July, the kinetics paused around the turn of the month. Brent fell 11.3% in a single session to $85.87 - the largest one-day decline since the April 8 ceasefire - and kept falling as the diplomacy took a durable shape: Iran negotiating with Oman, not Washington, on a "Malacca Strait-style" managed safe route through Hormuz; Secretary Rubio confirming August 4 there has been "progress made... but not finality" and expressing hope an agreement "will happen very shortly"; the President saying Wednesday a deal to reopen the strait could come "as early as Wednesday" (CNN live coverage, Aug 2-4; Yahoo Finance, Aug 5). WTI now hovers near $75, a full round trip from the $91+ prints of three weeks ago. Equities threw a party on it: the Dow set record closes Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday - five straight winning days into Thursday's -464 pullback - while gold spiked to its highest since June 18 and Seoul's Kospi, down 25% from its June peak three weeks ago, rebounded to a 3.8% single-day gain (CNBC, Aug 3-6).

The second regime change got a fraction of the attention and matters more for this book: Texas froze its own large-load queue. On August 3, Governor Abbott ordered the PUCT and ERCOT to audit "all data center projects currently advancing through ERCOT's interconnection process" before any additional approvals. ERCOT responded with Market Notice M-A080326-01: the Batch Zero classification letters scheduled for today - the largest single repricing event on the Texas powered-land calendar, per Vol. 19's framing - will not go out. ERCOT will seek a good-cause exception at the PUCT's August 20 open meeting (Troutman Pepper Locke client alert, Aug 4; ERCOT notice). Roughly 205 GW of study-eligible load, from a 438,000 MW queue that is ~89% data centers against a 92 GW all-time system peak, now waits on a political process whose next scheduled act is a commission meeting and whose next legislature convenes in January 2027 with expanded data-center authority on the agenda.

Between those two regime changes sat the heaviest earnings fortnight of the cycle, and it resolved the question Kimi K3 posed in July. The efficiency shock did not cut a single capex budget. Alphabet raised 2026 guidance to $195-205B (July 22) and was sold for it; Microsoft guided ~$175B and rose 8-9%; Meta guided $130-145B and fell 9-10%; Amazon printed its first $200B revenue quarter with $173B of trailing capex and rose 8-9% (company IR, Jul 22-30). GE Vernova, the physical layer's registrar, printed the other side of that ledger: 16 GW of net new gas commitments in one quarter, prices 20%+ above late 2025, and a production ramp that tops out at 30 GW a year in 2030 against demand already contracted past it. And on August 5, TPL - the purest public instrument on Far West Texas land and water - reported record everything and formally disclosed its role providing land and water to Project Kilby.

# II. TEXAS REGULATORY SHOCK - The Audit, the Suspension, and What Still Moves

**The corrected money mechanics.** Per the underlying rule (16 TAC §25.194): non-refundable interconnection fee of $50,000/MW plus study fees of $100,000-$300,000 by project size, with financial security posted at the intermediate-agreement stage (cash, IG parent guaranty, or LC). This supersedes the $100k/MW-plus-$100k/MW stack carried with a verification flag in Vols. 19-20; the correct statement is fee $50k/MW under the rule, security separate and stage-based. The flag is now resolved against the rule text (MLQ summary of §25.194; Keentel guide, data point).

**Scale restated.** The queue reached ~438,000 MW - more than five times the all-time system peak of ~85,500 MW (2026 peak running ~92 GW) - with ~89-90% data centers. Roughly 205 GW cleared study-eligibility screens for Batch Zero. ERCOT's own data shows the majority of requests claim operational dates by 2030. The gap between that queue and any buildable reality is the speculation the batch process was designed to price out - and the audit is Austin deciding the pricing mechanism itself needs a political sign-off first.


**Who sits in the limbo, concretely.** The suspension catches every named grid-serving project this briefing tracks: the Longhorn Data Center Abilene configuration (51 simple-cycle turbines, 1,759 MW, in the 59182 catalog) and its Stargate-corridor peers; every Permian-adjacent queue position marketed on an August 7 letter; the ~9 GW of large load already approved-to-energize whose successors now have no classification date; and, at one remove, every generator (CPV Basin Ranch class) whose revenue model includes co-located load it can no longer schedule. Explicitly outside the limbo: Kilby (off-grid, TCEQ-permitted, no ERCOT application), the Microsoft Reeves campus to the extent configured BTM, mobile-power patterns of the Pembrook type, and WLPUN structures whose paired generation cleared the July 10 gate - their allocation waits, but their physical build does not. The audit's scope language ("all data center projects currently advancing through ERCOT's interconnection process") is the operative boundary: the process is the exposure.

**The paper-stress channel.** Troutman's client note names the mechanism: milestones, financing drawdowns, and land closings keyed to the August 7 date now trigger change-in-law, force-majeure, and termination reviews across project documents. That is how a two-week administrative pause becomes a repricing even if the PUCT waves it through on the 20th - option windows expire, deposits go hard or don't, and counterparties reprice certainty. The first public marks from that stress are the Q4 distressed-comp watch this briefing opened in Vol. 19, now with a second catalyst.
