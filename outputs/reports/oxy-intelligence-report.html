SECTION 2

The Berkshire Hathaway financing relationship

Berkshire's exposure to OXY has four distinct layers — an $8.5B preferred, ~83.9M warrants, a ~27% common stake, and (since Jan 2026) the OxyChem operating business it bought outright. The preferred is the structural anchor and the most misunderstood piece, so its exact terms are set out below.

1) The 2019 preferred stock — $10B at 8%

  • To fund the 2019 Anadarko acquisition, Berkshire bought 100,000 shares of cumulative perpetual preferred at $100,000 liquidation value each = $10.0B, carrying an 8% annual dividend (cash, or in-kind at OXY's option). At ~$8.5B remaining, the dividend burden is ~$680M/yr.
  • Mandatory partial redemption triggers whenever OXY's trailing-12-month common distributions (dividends + buybacks) exceed $4.00/share; the triggered amount is redeemed at a 10% premium (110% of face).
  • Optional redemption of the entire balance begins August 2029 at 105% — independent of the $4.00 trigger.

2) Redemption progress — only ~$1.5B retired, all in 2023

Remaining liquidation value ≈ $8.5B (carrying value $8,287M at 31 Mar 2026). OXY's stated plan, per CFO Sunil Mathew on the Q1-2026 call, is to first reach $10B principal debt, then build cash to redeem the preferred at the first penalty-free window:

"…build cash on the balance sheet to redeem the preferred in August of 2029, when we can redeem the preferred without the $4 per share return of capital trigger." — Sunil Mathew, CFO, OXY Q1-2026 call, 6 May 2026

Redeeming the preferred is expected to improve cash flow ~$1.2B/yr vs. 2025. Note the circularity flagged by analysts: Berkshire's $9.7B OxyChem purchase helps OXY de-lever, while Berkshire separately still holds the ~$8.5B preferred OXY will eventually redeem at 105%. OxyChem cash is going to debt, not the preferred. (Sources: OXY Q3-2023 release; Q1-2026 call/transcript, 6 May 2026; Berkshire 10-Qs via Morningstar; stockanalysis.com.)

3) Warrants
  • 83,858,824 (~83.9M) warrants on OXY common at $59.624/share (originally $62.50, adjusted for dividends).
  • Unexercised; expire one year after the preferred is fully redeemed. Full exercise would lift Berkshire to ~33.8% of common.
4) Common stake & FERC
  • 264,941,431 common shares — flat since 7 Feb 2025; 28.1% (30 Jun 2025), ~26.6% now after warrant-driven share-count dilution. Cost basis ~low-$50s. Last buy 7 Feb 2025.
  • FERC authorized up to 50% (order 19 Aug 2022, docket EC22-87-000) — driven by shared Louisiana FERC-jurisdictional grid assets.

Case study · Pecos County

Century gas/CO₂ plant — the largest single-source industrial CO₂ capture in North America

Under a June 2008 agreement, SandRidge built and operates the plant while OXY funded construction (~$1.1B) and owns the CO₂-handling facilities, taking 100% of the extracted CO₂ for Permian EOR (SandRidge keeps and sells the separated methane). Inlet treating capacity is 800 MMcf/d of high-CO₂ Val Verde gas, built in two trains (Train 1 started 26 Sep 2010; Train 2 in 2011). At full rate it captures ~8.4–8.7 Mt CO₂/yr (~450 MMcf/d takeaway, +~50 MMcf/d from legacy SandRidge plants), moved ~160 mi via McCamey to the Denver City hub. OXY projected the stream would lift Permian output up to ~50,000 boe/d. A billion-dollar OXY CO₂-capture/processing complex inside Pecos County.

Sources: Oil & Gas Journal (plant start-up); SandRidge release, 30 Jun 2008; MIT Sequestration; GEM. "800 MMcf/d" is inlet treating capacity; "~450 MMcf/d" is CO₂ takeaway — different metrics, both correct.

Case study · CO₂-EOR network

The Denver City CO₂ hub-and-spoke grid

OXY runs >3.5 Tcf of CO₂ through Permian EOR and anchors a CO₂ grid centered on the Denver City hub (Gaines/Yoakum Cos., TX), fed by natural and anthropogenic CO₂. Bravo Dome (Harding/Union Cos., NE New Mexico) is a ~99%-pure natural CO₂ field, OXY-operated, feeding the Bravo Pipeline (218 mi, 20-in, 382 MMcf/d) to Denver City and on into the Slaughter (Cochran/Hockley) and Wasson (Yoakum) units. Correction to a common error: the ~500-mi Cortez Pipeline (McElmo Dome, CO → Denver City; ~1.3–1.5 Bcf/d) is Kinder Morgan-operated (KM 50% / ExxonMobil 37% / Cortez Vickers 13%) — not an OXY asset; OXY participates only as a CO₂ shipper/buyer. Century (above) supplies the industrial-capture CO₂ leg of the same grid.

Sources: GEM (Bravo Dome & pipeline); Kinder Morgan CO₂ ops; Four Corners Free Press.

Case study · Midstream equity

Western Midstream (WES) — OXY's retained midstream engine

OXY holds ~41.9% of WES as of YE-2025 (39.7% LP + 2.2% GP) and controls it through the 100%-owned general partner; OXY supplied ~60% of WES revenue in 2025. (The widely cited "~49%" is dated — a 3 Feb 2026 redemption of 15.3M units (~$610M) alongside a Delaware gas-contract reset stepped OXY to ~39.5%.) WES's Delaware core (DBM) spans Culberson/Loving/Reeves/Ward (TX) and Eddy/Lea (NM), scaling gas processing to ~2,490 MMcf/d. OXY has explored a full WES exit (stake ~$20B, JPMorgan advising) to cut debt; no sale has closed — confirm ownership before treating WES as a fixed OXY asset. WES's produced-water build-out is covered in §5.

Sources: WES 2025 10-K (filed 18 Feb 2026); SEC 13D/A & 8-K (Jan–Feb 2026); Hart Energy.

Case study · Transaction

The Enterprise gathering sale (Aug 2025) — OXY converts owner → shipper

OXY sold its Midland-Basin gas-gathering affiliate to Enterprise Products for $580M cash, debt-free (signed 6 Aug, closed 22 Aug 2025): ~200 mi of gathering across a ~73,000-acre, four-county dedication (>1,000 locations; the four counties were not named in the releases). OXY simultaneously signed a long-term G&P agreement keeping its gas dedicated to Enterprise, which is building the Athena cryogenic plant (300 MMcf/d, 40,000 bbl/d NGL, in-service Q4-2026). OXY retains no gathering here — the rationale was deleveraging after the $12B CrownRock acquisition. OXY's earlier crude-terminal asset, the Oxy Ingleside Energy Center (San Patricio Co.), was likewise divested (to Moda 2018 → Enbridge $3.0B, Oct 2021).

Sources: Enterprise/BusinessWire (6 Aug 2025); OGJ; Rigzone (close, 27 Aug 2025); OGJ/Enbridge (Ingleside).

Case study · Recycling network

South Curtis Ranch & Lost Tank — OXY's named recycle-into-completions footprint

OXY's disclosed, operator-attributable recycling capacity sits in a long partnership with Select Water Solutions (NYSE: WTTR) across both sub-basins. South Curtis Ranch (Midland Basin), operational since March 2021, surpassed 50 MM bbl treated and reused by May 2024 (Select cites the partnership reaching ~97 MM bbl combined by Q1-2026), cycling recycled water back into OXY completions to displace freshwater; daily capacity and the specific county are not disclosed. Lost Tank (Lea Co., NM), launched 2022, scaled to up to ~180,000 bbl/d with 1.9 MM bbl storage and a 13-mi pipeline into OXY's "Mesa Verde" development area, reaching 50 MM bbl recycled by Sept 2024. Two basins, one vendor — a direct hedge against the disposal-curtailment risk below.

Sources: Select Water Solutions releases (20 May 2024; Sept 2024); selectwater.com.

Case study · Desalination

WES produced-water desalination pilots (JIP)

OXY's only disclosed Permian desalination activity runs through Western Midstream's joint-industry program. JIP 2 (Reeves Co., near Red Bluff Reservoir) started up 17 Jun 2026, taking 2,000 bbl/d of produced water and yielding ~1,000 bbl/d of reclaimed freshwater (10× the 2023 JIP 1 pilot) targeted at industrial cooling, irrigation and surface discharge, to guide commercial-scale design. The Oct-2025 Aris acquisition explicitly added "beneficial reuse and desalination" expertise WES intends to accelerate. Permian produced water is hypersaline (~120,000–130,000+ mg/L TDS vs. seawater ~35,000); brine management (ZLD/crystallization) is the core unsolved cost driver, and the Texas Produced Water Consortium target is treatment to <200 TDS at ≤$1/bbl.

Sources: WES release, 17 Jun 2026; TxPWC 2024 report; TWDB desalination cost study.

Case study · Lithium / brine

TerraLithium direct lithium extraction (DLE) — capability, with a scope boundary

TerraLithium is a wholly-owned OXY subsidiary commercializing DLE: it strips lithium chloride from hypersaline brine and converts it to battery-grade product without evaporation ponds, returning spent brine — functionally a brine-treatment train. The IP traces to Simbol Materials via OXY's 2022 acquisition of All-American Lithium. On 25 Jun 2024 OXY and BHE Renewables (Berkshire Hathaway Energy) formed a 50/50 JV to deploy DLE at BHE's ten geothermal plants in California's Imperial Valley / Salton Sea; TerraLithium retains worldwide licensing rights. Scope boundary: all concrete TerraLithium activity is California geothermal brine — there is no announced Permian/Texas produced-water DLE facility; OXY frames lithium-from-oilfield-brine as strategic intent only. Deal value, plant capacity and start-up date are not disclosed.

Sources: OXY TerraLithium page; OXY release & Reuters, 25 Jun 2024; Mining.com.

Regulatory context shaping OXY's water strategy

  • RRC overhaul: Statewide Rule 8 replaced by 16 TAC Chapter 4 (effective 1 Jul 2025) — operators may recycle produced water for completions without a permit, subject to siting rules.
  • Surface discharge: SB 1145 (effective 1 Sep 2025) moved permitting of treated-produced-water land application/surface discharge from RRC to TCEQ; HB 49 expanded reuse liability protection; TCEQ rulemaking is in progress (targeted adoption 2027).
  • Seismicity / disposal curtailment — the structural recycling driver: three Permian Seismic Response Areas (Gardendale; Northern Culberson-Reeves — all deep permits suspended Jan 2024, re-suspended after a M5.4 quake May 2025; Stanton — ~84,000 bbl/d cut), plus a tightened SWD permitting regime (effective 1 Jun 2025).

Case study · NET Power (NYSE: NPWR) — OXY owns 46.46%

The carbon-capture-on-gas template — and its 2026 pivot

The technology. NET Power commercializes the Allam-Fetvedt cycle: natural gas burned with pure oxygen using high-pressure supercritical CO₂ as the working fluid, so the exhaust is a nearly pure, pipeline-pressure CO₂ stream — capture is inherent, not bolted on (~97% capture, near-zero air emissions, air-cooled / very low water). The proof point is the La Porte, TX demonstration plant (50 MWth; first fire 2018; first ERCOT grid power 16 Nov 2021).

The flagship — and its escalation. The first utility-scale plant ("Project Permian" / SN1), on an OXY-leased site near Odessa (Ector Co.), was originally ~300 MW Allam-cycle (Baker Hughes turboexpander; Air Liquide ASU; Zachry FEED), with OXY providing the site (lease Q1-2024), CO₂ transport/sequestration, and power offtake. Cost escalated from ~$750–950M to ~$1B (Nov 2023) to $1.7–2.0B+ (Mar 2025), and the schedule slipped from 2026 toward 2027–28.

The pivot (YE-2025 update, 9 Mar 2026): NET Power paused the Allam-cycle 300 MW design and re-scoped the same site to an 80 MW plant using Siemens gas turbines + Entropy post-combustion capture (PCC) — i.e., a conventional turbine with bolt-on amine capture, not the inherent-capture Allam cycle. FID targeted 2H-2026; commercial operations ~early 2029. The Allam-cycle commercialization is deferred, not cancelled; OXY's host-site and CO₂-offtake role persist.

Ownership. NET Power went public via the Rice Acquisition Corp. II SPAC (June 2023; NYSE: NPWR; ~$1.5B EV). OXY is the largest shareholder at 46.46% (held via OLCV; +$250M follow-on), alongside 8 Rivers (~22%), Constellation and Baker Hughes.

The data-center template — stated plainly. A NET Power-style plant delivers firm, 24/7, low-/zero-emission gas baseload with the CO₂ captured for sequestration or EOR, air-cooled (minimal water), behind-the-meter, on fast red-state siting that bypasses multi-year grid queues — the profile AI data centers are seeking (CEO Danny Rice: "high demand for clean baseload power into next decade"). Caveat: there is no primary OXY/NET Power statement pairing the Odessa plant with a named hyperscaler — the data-center linkage is the category thesis, not a signed contract. OXY does hold gas gathering across the same West-Texas geography as several third-party data-center developments (factual proximity only; no specific third-party project is an OXY power template).

Sources: NET Power Q4/YE-2025 results & business update (9 Mar 2026); POWER Mag; E&E News; Power Engineering; BusinessWire (SPAC, 8 Jun 2023); Energy Intelligence (46.46%); Oxy Goldsmith release (Oct 2019).

SECTION 7

Carbon capture (1PointFive) — context & economics

Included for completeness, but right-sized: direct air capture is barely economic today — a subsidy- and premium-buyer-dependent business, not a near-term value driver. 1PointFive is OXY's wholly-owned DAC subsidiary, built on Carbon Engineering technology (acquired ~$1.1B, closed Nov 2023).

Projects

  • STRATOS (Ector Co., TX): up to 500,000 t CO₂/yr; ~$1.3B; BlackRock $550M JV (~40%); EPA Class VI permits 7 Apr 2025; online targeted Q2-2026 after repeated delays (from end-2024/2025).
  • South Texas DAC hub / King Ranch (Kleberg Co.): DOE award up to $500M (Sep 2024, $50M initial); initial 500,000 t/yr → >1M, site potential ~30M t/yr; ADNOC/XRG to consider up to $500M (May 2025); Class VI pending. Pre-construction.

DAC economics — candid

Cost today runs ~$385–690/t (WRI avg ~$490/t). The 45Q credit pays $180/t for DAC + saline storage ($130/t DAC+EOR) vs $85/t for point-source. Voluntary durable-CDR prices run ~$200–350/t. The math — ~$400–600+/t cost minus ~$180/t credit — leaves a ~$220–420/t gap closeable only by stacking 45Q with top-of-market voluntary buyers. No official STRATOS per-tonne cost is disclosed.

Caveats, data gaps & verification flags

Each item below is flagged because it is either not publicly disclosed, recently changed, or commonly mis-stated — and each is material enough that relying on a wrong version would mislead.

  • NET Power Odessa plant is no longer 300 MW Allam-cycle. Why it's a caveat: the YE-2025 update (Mar 2026) re-scoped it to 80 MW (Siemens GT + Entropy post-combustion capture); all 2022–2024 "300 MW Allam SN1" reporting is superseded. Using the old figure overstates both scale and the "inherent capture" technology.
  • OXY's WES stake is ~41.9% (YE-2025), not "49%." Why: unit redemptions (incl. 3 Feb 2026) stepped it down; OXY also may sell the WES stake entirely (~$20B, explored). Treating WES as a fixed ~half-owned OXY asset would be wrong on both count and durability.
  • Cortez Pipeline is not an OXY asset. Why: it is Kinder Morgan-operated (KM 50% / Exxon 37%); OXY is only a CO₂ shipper. The Bravo Pipeline/Dome (NE New Mexico) is OXY-operated — the two are routinely conflated.
  • TerraLithium DLE is California-only. Why: there is no announced Permian produced-water DLE facility; the Permian-lithium angle is stated intent, not an operating asset.
  • DAC is barely economic. Why: ~$400–600+/t cost vs ~$180/t max 45Q credit; the business depends on subsidy-stacking and premium voluntary buyers. Even Buffett's 2023 letter calls the economic feasibility "yet to be proven."
  • The Berkshire preferred is largely un-redeemed. Why: only ~$1.5B of $10B retired (all 2023); ~$8.5B remains at an ~$680M/yr 8% cost; redemption is deferred to August 2029. OxyChem proceeds went to debt, not the preferred.
