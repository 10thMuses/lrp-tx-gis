## I. Executive Summary

**The tenant question is answered: it's Amazon, and it owns the dirt.** The single largest open item in the June 12 memo — no named offtaker, no public record of land tenure — resolved in the first week of August 2026. Amazon filed three data center construction registrations with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) at 6000 TX-18, Pecos County (the GW Ranch site, ~11–17 mi north of Fort Stockton), and when the filings were surfaced publicly (Cleanview/Distilled, Aug 7), **Amazon confirmed it has acquired the 8,000-acre GW Ranch site** and published an official statement on the project. Deal structure per Amazon and trade press: **Amazon owns the site and campus; Pacifico Energy remains the power developer and will sell Amazon power** from the permitted 7.65 GW gas + 1.8 GW BESS + 750 MWac solar complex.

**The abatement moved too.** The June 22, 2026 Pecos County Commissioners Court agenda carried the public hearing and resolution to **designate the GW Ranch Reinvestment Zone (Tax Code Ch. 312)** and to **execute a tax abatement agreement with Pacifico GW LLC** — the executed-abatement milestone the June memo flagged as the FID-intent signal. (Executed agreement text not yet located in public registries; agenda items are the record to date.)

**Construction is visibly underway.** Airbus satellite imagery dated July 24, 2026 shows land clearing at the site; the TDLR filings show construction windows of **August–December 2026** for the first three buildings at **$300M each ($900M initial data center shell capex)**.

**What this changes:** the June memo's verdict was "a permitted development option — tenant-contingent and pre-capitalized." Two of the three falsification conditions have now partially triggered: an investment-grade anchor (Amazon) is in, and the Ch. 312 abatement reached execution stage. Project financing and the turbine schedule remain undisclosed, but with Amazon as owner the capital constraint is largely mooted — Amazon self-funds shells and can backstop the power buildout. GW Ranch has flipped from "distressed-option watch" to **the most credible GW-class project in the Permian**.

**What is still not filed anywhere public:** an ERCOT interconnection request (Amazon says it is "actively exploring" grid transition; no queue entry disclosed as of Aug 17 press), an RRC T-4 permit for the 15-mile Waha lateral, a Middle Pecos GCD water-well permit, and any Pacifico project-financing or EPC mandate.

**Takeaway:** this is the Longfellow/Poolside inverse — instead of a thin-credit tenant leasing from a land developer, the hyperscaler took the land itself and left the developer holding the permit and the power contract. Amazon carrying the site eliminates tenant-credit risk and most of the $12B financing question; Pacifico's residual position (permit + PPA + abatement) is now effectively Amazon-backstopped.
