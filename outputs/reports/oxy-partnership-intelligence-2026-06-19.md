# Occidental Petroleum (OXY) — Partnership Intelligence Brief

**CONFIDENTIAL — LRP / CARAMBA NORTH · OXY COLLABORATION**

**Prepared:** 2026-06-19 · **Prepared by:** Land Resource Partners · **Subject:** Occidental Petroleum Corporation (NYSE: OXY) — portfolio, Permian/Pecos footprint, data-center–adjacent activities, and counterparty briefing, in support of a proposed strategic collaboration arising from the Caramba North mineral/surface negotiation · **Classification:** Confidential — internal & principal use

> **Context.** LRP advises the Clayton Williams family on repurposing ~1,300 acres in Pecos County (Caramba North; tract centroid ≈ 30.9032° N, 102.9747° W) for hyperscale data-center use. Site control for a data center requires controlling the **shallow mineral estate** (0–5,000 ft, in some buyer specs 0–3,000 ft) to remove drilling/vibration risk, and controlling the **working interest** (surface drilling rights). On the Williams farm the **mineral estate is owned by Occidental (OXY)** and the **working interest by Chevron**. In an initial call, OXY declined to give a cheap surface waiver — correctly noting a data-center site runs ~$40B all-in per GW, making the mineral/surface line item immaterial to the hyperscaler's capital stack — and instead proposed a **broader collaboration**: trading market intelligence and pairing LRP's land/data-center origination with OXY's basin-wide minerals, working interests, fee lands, CCS/carbon capture, produced-water desalination, and power-generation capabilities. This brief assesses what OXY brings.

## 1. Executive thesis

OXY's move on the call — declining a cheap surface waiver, then proposing to **partner, trade intelligence, and collaborate across CCS, water, and power** — is the right read of the situation, and the partnership is genuinely high-value: **OXY is the single most multi-dimensional counterparty available in this exact geography.** The same LRP map built for the Caramba vibration thesis independently shows OXY operating as a generator, a 138 kV grid operator *in Pecos County*, a gas-plant owner in Pecos and Crane, a CO₂/CCS operator, and a basin-wide E&P (§5).

**Why OXY is additive (in priority order):**
1. **Carbon management is the marquee.** OXY's **1PointFive** runs the world's only utility-scale DAC plant (**STRATOS**, Ector County) and the largest **hyperscaler carbon-removal contract on record (Microsoft, 500k t)**, plus the **King Ranch DAC hub** (DOE + ADNOC backing). It lets a Pecos hyperscaler buy a packaged **"net-zero data center"**: behind-the-meter gas power + point-source CCS + DAC offtake. **Google and Meta have not yet signed 1PointFive** — an opening LRP relationships can help close (§3.1).
2. **In-basin power + CCS infrastructure, literally in the county.** OXY's **~$1.1B Century gas/CO₂ complex sits inside Pecos County** (800 MMcf/d; largest single-source industrial CO₂ capture in North America), with a CO₂ pipeline spine (Bravo/Cortez) ready for CCS-on-power. Its gas-gathering feeds the **Project Horizon** gas-to-power template on Longfellow Ranch — also Pecos (§3.3, §4).
3. **Produced-water treatment/desalination** (TerraLithium DLE; WES's Reeves-County desal pilot) is the path to **non-potable cooling water** in water-scarce Pecos (§3.2).

**Strategic-patience advantage.** OXY is **Berkshire-anchored** (~27% of common + $8.5B preferred) and has cut debt from ~$23B to **~$13.3B**. It does **not** need a quick mineral monetization — it can structure a **multi-decade** surface/infrastructure arrangement, which is exactly what a hyperscale ground lease needs (§2, §4).

**Three caveats to price in (each flagged in-text):**
- **OxyChem is divested** — sold to Berkshire ($9.7B, closed Jan 2026). The chemicals/water-treatment-chemical angle now runs through Berkshire, not OXY (§3.5).
- **Western Midstream may be sold** — confirm OXY still controls WES before counting its water/pipeline/ROW network as an "OXY asset" (§3.4).
- **OXY is not a TPL-style West-Texas fee empire.** Its big inherited land-grant mineral estate was in the Rockies (sold 2020); the famous West-Texas railroad fee belongs to **TPL**, and OXY has been a **net seller** in the TX Delaware. Your title work that **OXY owns the Williams-tract minerals** stands — but treat OXY as a *targeted* owner here, not a county-wide fee holder. **The partnership value is OXY's capabilities + the specific Williams ownership, not basin-wide Pecos mineral breadth** — which is why the "position in Pecos" map layer (§8, Layer B) should be built from actual county/RRC ownership records, exactly as you proposed.

**Counterparties** (§7): all three OXY contacts sit on the **Land / commercial** side — the right people for a minerals-and-surface deal. **John Cranfill** (Sr. Director, BD — US Onshore Resources & **Carbon Management**) is the strategic node bridging OXY's upstream and its CCUS agenda.

## 3. Where OXY is additive to a data-center partnership

*OXY's pitch — "partner up, trade intelligence, collaborate on CCS, produced-water desalination, and power" — maps onto five capability areas below. ### 3.1 Carbon capture / CCS / Direct Air Capture (1PointFive) — the marquee asset

This is the single most strategically relevant thing OXY brings to a hyperscale deal, because **1PointFive's entire commercial thesis is now anchored to AI/hyperscaler emissions demand.**

- **1PointFive / Oxy Low Carbon Ventures** — OXY's carbon-management subsidiary (now led by **Richard Jackson**, promoted to SVP & COO Oct 2025); publicly targets a fleet of up to **70 DAC facilities by 2035**. ([Oxy](https://www.oxy.com/news/news-releases/occidental-promotes-richard-a.-jackson-to-senior-vice-president-and-chief-operating-officer))
- **STRATOS DAC (Ector County, near Odessa)** — the **world's largest** Direct Air Capture plant: **up to 500,000 t CO₂/yr** (scalable to 1 Mt), **~$1.3B**, EPC **Worley**, technology **Carbon Engineering**, built via a **JV with BlackRock ($550M, announced 2023-11-07)**. Secured the **first EPA Class VI permits ever issued for a DAC project** (saline sequestration) in **April 2025**; ramping to commercial start. ### 3.2 Produced-water treatment & desalination

Data centers need large volumes of **non-potable cooling water**; in water-scarce Pecos County, treated **produced water** is the only scalable source that avoids competing for Edwards-Trinity/Pecos Valley freshwater. The Permian produced **>20 MMbbl/d of water in 2024, projected >26 MMbbl/d by 2030** ([JPT/SPE](https://jpt.spe.org/twa/balancing-growth-and-risk-why-water-management-is-the-permian-basins-biggest-challenge)). OXY's capability:

- **South Curtis Ranch** recycling (Midland Basin, with Select Water Solutions) — **>50 MM bbl treated/recycled** since March 2021 ([PR Newswire 5/2024](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/permian-water-recycling-facility-exceeds-50-million-barrels-treated-and-reused-302150445.html)); **Lost Tank** (Lea Co., NM) up to **180,000 bbl/d**.
- **TerraLithium** — OXY subsidiary (Carbon Engineering/Calgary lineage) with patented direct-lithium-extraction (brine-treatment) tech; **JV with BHE Renewables (June 2024)** to commercialize. The DLE train is fundamentally a brine-desalination process **directly transferable to producing non-potable cooling makeup from produced water.** ([Oxy](https://www.oxy.com/news/news-releases/occidental-and-bhe-renewables-form-joint-venture-to-commercialize-terralithium-extraction-technology/))
- **WES Reeves County desalination pilot** reclaimed ~**1,000 bbl/d of freshwater** from produced water — a commercial-scale proof point in a county abutting Pecos.
- *Caveat: no public OXY produced-water **desalination** pilot in Pecos County specifically is yet on record — the capability is adjacent (DLE + WES pilots), not a deployed Pecos product. A pilot here would be a natural joint first project.*

### 3.3 Power generation

- Owned generation is **thin post-OxyChem**: **Goldsmith Solar** (Ector Co., **16 MW**, 2019) powers EOR. The 380.7 MW Battleground cogen departed with the OxyChem sale.
- The real lever is **gas-to-power off OXY's gathering systems.** The directly-analogous precedent is **in the target county**: **Project Horizon**, a 2 GW AI campus on **Longfellow Ranch, Pecos County**, planned on-site aeroderivative turbines **fueled directly from existing Occidental and Targa gathering systems**, targeting up to **2.5 GW of bridge generation within ~18 months** (announced Oct 2025). *Caveat: the CoreWeave anchor lease was terminated in early 2026 and the deal stalled — so treat it as a **proven template, not a live reference** for OXY-gas-feeds-behind-the-meter-turbines.* ([Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/machine-learning/article/55326541/how-coreweave-and-poolside-are-teaming-up-in-west-texas-to-build-the-next-generation-of-ai-data-centers); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ai-startup-poolside-teams-up-with-coreweave-on-2gw-data-center-in-texas/))

### 3.4 Midstream, pipelines & rights-of-way (Western Midstream)

- **Western Midstream Partners (WES)** — OXY owns ~**49% + the general partner.** WES's Delaware Basin **produced-water system handles ~3 MMbbl/d** (recycle up to ~600k bbl/d), materially scaled by the **~$2.0B Aris Water Solutions acquisition (Oct 2025: +~1,800 Mbbl/d handling, +790 mi water pipeline, +625,000 dedicated acres)**. The **Pathfinder pipeline** (42 mi, 30-in, **>800 Mbbl/d**, in service 2027-01-01) anchors eastern Loving County. ([WES](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/western-midstream-announces-pathfinder-pipeline-expansion-of-delaware-basin-produced-water-system-and-2025-guidance-302386231.html))
- **Why it matters:** WES's water-gathering, recycling, pipeline corridors and **ROW across Reeves/Ward/Loving/Pecos** are precisely the rights-of-way and volume a DC water/gas supply needs.
- **Caveat (material):** OXY has explored **selling its WES stake (~$18–20B+)** to cut its ~$18.5B debt — **confirm current ownership/sale status before relying on WES as an "OXY asset."** Aris/Pathfinder are also weighted to the **Delaware (Loving/Reeves), north of Pecos.** ([Reuters via BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/occidental-explores-sale-of-western-midstream-stake-reuters-reports-1.2036919))

### 3.5 OxyChem — **divested; no longer an OXY capability**

OXY sold its **entire chemicals segment to Berkshire Hathaway for $9.7B** (announced 2025-10-02, closed **2026-01-02**) — 14 chlor-alkali/PVC sites, two cogen plants, and the 50% Ingleside ethylene-cracker interest; OXY retained only legacy environmental liabilities. For reference the franchise earned ~**$1.1B on $4.9B sales (2024)**. **Any chemicals / water-treatment-chemical angle now runs through Berkshire, not OXY** — discount it from the OXY value case. ## 4. OXY's footprint in Pecos & abutting counties

OXY is the **largest producer in the Permian Basin**, with **~2.9 million net acres in Texas & New Mexico** (~1.5M unconventional Delaware/Midland + ~1.4M conventional) and standing as the **Permian's largest CO₂-EOR operator** — **34 CO₂-EOR projects**, storing up to **20 Mt CO₂/yr** underground. ([oxy.com/operations](https://www.oxy.com/operations/performance-production/)) Its conventional West-Texas position traces largely to the **2000 acquisition of Altura Energy** (the BP Amoco/Shell Permian JV) for **$3.6B** — 850 MMboe, ~6,400 wells, then the largest oil producer in Texas — held today under **OXY USA Inc. / OXY USA WTP LP**, the entities that also carry legacy **fee surface and minerals** in the Pecos/Reeves/Crane corridor. ([GlobeNewswire 4/19/2000](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2000/04/19/278333/6994/en/Occidental-Completes-Acquisition-of-Altura-Energy.html))

> **Scale check (important for "their position in Pecos").** Do **not** model OXY as a TPL-style West-Texas fee empire. OXY's large *inherited land-grant mineral estate* (Anadarko/Union Pacific checkerboard) was in **Wyoming/Colorado/Utah** and was **sold to Orion Mine Finance for ~$1.33B in 2020** ([E&MJ](https://www.e-mj.com/breaking-news/occidental-sells-land-grant-assets-to-orion/)); the famous *West-Texas* railroad fee belongs to **Texas Pacific Land Corp**, not OXY. OXY has also been a **net seller in the TX Delaware (Pecos/Reeves/Ward)** — to Colgate (2021, $508M) and Permian Resources (2024, ~$818M). Your title work that **OXY owns the Williams-tract minerals** is the operative fact and stands; the caution is only against extrapolating it to a sweeping county-wide position. **A precise public fee-acreage figure is not disclosed — the §8 Layer B build (county/RRC ownership records) is how to quantify OXY's actual Pecos position.**

## 6. Deal comps & negotiation framing

**Legal backdrop (controls the structure).** In Texas the **mineral estate is dominant**: the mineral owner (OXY) has the implied right to use as much surface as reasonably necessary to develop minerals. The **accommodation doctrine** is a weak shield for a surface developer — and, per ***Lyle v. Midway Solar*** (El Paso Ct. App., 2021), decided on a **315-acre Pecos County** solar tract, it provides **no remedy until the mineral owner actually attempts to develop** (*"If the Lyles are not exercising their right, there is nothing to be accommodated"*). **Implication:** a hyperscale buyer cannot rely on the accommodation doctrine; they require a **negotiated surface waiver / shallow-rights subordination** from **OXY (minerals)** and **Chevron (working interest)** — which is precisely the LRP–OXY deal. Title underwriters and project financiers typically **require** such a waiver/subordination to fund. ([Texas Ag Law — Lyle v. Midway](https://agrilife.org/texasaglaw/2021/01/25/case-addresses-solar-lease-mineral-owner-and-the-accommodation-doctrine/); [Farella — solar vs. mineral estate](https://www.fbm.com/publications/achieving-compatibility-between-solar-project-developers-and-mineral-estate-holders/))

**Most on-point comp — OXY is *already* contributing surface to a Permian data-center power project.** Chevron's first AI-data-center power complex (unveiled at its **2025-11-12** investor day) — **2.5 GW scaling to 5 GW, ~$7B, online 2027**, with **GE Vernova / Engine No. 1** and **Microsoft** as prospective offtaker — sits on a ~**7,000-acre** site in the **Pecos/Reeves** area on land reportedly contributed by **Texas Pacific Land, Oxy USA, and WaterBridge.** *(The Oxy-USA land-contribution detail is from a single secondary analyst source — verify in Reeves/Pecos county records or TPL filings before relying on it — but if confirmed it is the strongest possible precedent: OXY already monetizes fee surface into exactly this use.)* ([ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/61972-chevron-to-build-its-first-data-center-power-plant-in-texas-permian-area); [310value analysis](https://310value.substack.com/p/data-centers-in-the-permian-basin-9c9))

**Adjacent Pecos-County comps** (establish the county as a hyperscale magnet and the gas-to-power template): **Project Horizon** (Longfellow Ranch, 2 GW, 568 dev-ready acres, 8×250 MW, on a legacy **Occidental gas plant**) and **Pacifico GW Ranch** (7.65 GW) — both Pecos. ([Public Power](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/plans-unveiled-2-gw-artificial-intelligence-campus-west-texas); [Texas Tribune 2/2/2026](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/02/02/texas-permian-basin-power-plant-project-data-centers/))

**Mineral/surface valuation benchmarks:**
- **TPL (2024-08-27):** **$169M** for **4,106 net royalty acres** (Culberson Co., ≈ **$41,098/ac**) **+ 4,120 surface acres** (Martin Co., ≈ **$41,068/ac**) — a clean Permian mineral *and* surface benchmark. ([TPL](https://www.texaspacific.com/investors/news-events/press-releases/detail/161/texas-pacific-land-corporation-acquires-permian-mineral))
- **Producing Permian mineral rights** generally trade **$2,000–$8,000+/ac**, higher near active development. ([Mineral Royalties Group](https://mineralroyaltiesgroup.com/permian-basin-mineral-interests-for-sale/))
- **Surface-waiver / subordination structures** (solar/DC precedent): absolute surface waivers prohibiting mineral-owner surface disturbance, compensated via an **escalating (CPI-indexed) annual fee** or production royalty. No public *data-center shallow-rights* $/ac figure exists — **not yet publicly benchmarked.**

**Negotiation framing.** A hyperscale data center runs **~$40B all-in per GW**. Against the Chevron (~$7B / 2.5 GW) and Horizon/Pacifico comps, even a generous shallow-mineral (0–5,000 ft) buyout **plus** surface/working-interest waiver — priced at a multiple of the ~$41k/ac TPL benchmark — is a **rounding-error fraction of project capital.** OXY's own first call confirmed it understands this leverage (hence the move to a broader partnership rather than a cheap one-off waiver). **LRP's leverage is alignment, not the accommodation doctrine:** OXY is demonstrably already a willing surface/power participant in Permian data-center projects, and the partnership lets OXY capture CCS + power + water + CDR value across *many* such tracts, not just the Williams fee.
