What changed recently

  • Gas-fleet consolidation closed. NRG closed its $12.0B / 13 GW gas-fleet purchase from LS Power (Jan 30, 2026; ~$923/kW); Constellation closed its ~$26.6B Calpine acquisition (2026), forming the largest U.S. generator (~60 GW); AES agreed to a $33.4B take-private by GIP/EQT (Mar 2, 2026).
  • AI-lease stack repriced upward. Cipher–AWS Black Pearl ($5.5B / 15 yr / 300 MW) and Cipher–Fluidstack Barber Lake (→~$9B with extensions) anchor a $1.22–2.26M/MW‑yr band; hyperscaler credit backstops (Google, AWS) are now standard.
  • Nuclear went from restarts to fleets. Meta committed to up to 6.6 GW (Oklo/Vistra/TerraPower, Jan 9, 2026); DOE issued a $26.5B Southern Co. financing (Feb 2026) and $2.7B of enrichment task orders (Jan 6, 2026); Westinghouse’s $80B AP1000 fleet pledge (Oct 2025) implies ~$7,300/kW vs Vogtle’s realized ~$16,500/kW.
  • Capacity scarcity priced in. PJM’s 2026/2027 auction cleared at the $329.17/MW‑day cap; North-America solar/wind PPAs rose to $64.49 / $79.40/MWh (LevelTen Q1 2026).
  • Late-window (Jun 13–19, 2026): no major Texas land, lease, or fleet closings; activity was early-stage (Centrus–Oklo HALEU supply LOI Jun 18; CIM Grape 246 MWac solar+storage financing ~$600M Jun 18; a cluster of geothermal venture rounds).

A Land & Power Leases $/MW · $/MW‑yr · $/acre · $/AF → bbl/d

The lease contract is the fundamental unit of value in AI infrastructure: a hyperscaler or neocloud tenant commits 10–15 years of rent against a landlord’s powered shell, and that annual rent — $1.22–2.26 million per MW-year across executed deals — sets the asset’s worth more than the dirt beneath it. The defining 2025–26 feature is credit support: investment-grade backstops (Google for Cipher and TeraWulf; AWS as ultimate guarantor at Black Pearl) convert a former bitcoin miner’s lease into financeable, near-utility cash flow. Land trades at a wide spread — from ~$8,000/acre for raw municipal parcels bought ahead of the boom to $480,000/acre to buy out an already-powered site — and water, increasingly the binding constraint, is benchmarked here in barrels per day.

A.2  Credit support & lease structure

Google–Cipher: backstops $1.4B of Fluidstack’s obligations (rising to ~$1.73B with the Nov expansion) for ~24M warrants (~5.4% pro-forma). Google–TeraWulf: backstops $1.8B (then +$1.4B on the CB-5 expansion → ~$3.2B total) for ~41M warrants (~14%). AWS–Cipher: ultimate guarantor of Black Pearl rent (per $2B Black Pearl Compute LLC notes, Feb 2026, due 2031). Common terms: 10–15-yr initial, 3×5-yr extensions, ~3% annual escalators, triple-net. The backstops — not the miners’ own balance sheets — are what make these leases financeable.

A.3  Landlord contracted-revenue backlog

The miners’ pivot has produced multi-billion contracted backlogs — company-stated initial-term revenue, not market value. Applied Digital leads at ~$36B base ($86B with renewals; Jun 2026) across 1.4 GW / five campuses; Hut 8 reached $16.8B after its 352 MW Beacon Point lease; TeraWulf $12.8B (522 IT MW), Cipher ~$11.4B, Core Scientific ~$10.2B. (CoreWeave’s $9B bid for Core Scientific was terminated Oct 30 2025; CORZ remains standalone.)

B Power Generation Fleet M&A $/kW of capacity

Operating thermal fleets are changing hands at ~$900–1,320/kW — roughly 40–65% of new-build CCGT replacement cost (~$2,000–2,500/kW) — as buyers race to own existing, interconnected capacity rather than wait years to build it. NRG explicitly framed its LS Power buy at 50% of replacement cost; Talen and NRG both cited mid-single-digit EV/EBITDA multiples.

Read-through across the stack

One pattern recurs at every layer: the scarce, financeable unit is firm, interconnected capacity under long-term contract, and its price is climbing. An AI lease throws off $1.22–2.26M/MW-yr once a hyperscaler’s credit backstops it; an operating gas fleet trades at ~$900–1,320/kW, less than half of new-build; a nuclear restart delivers firm megawatts at ~$1,900/kW versus ~$16,500/kW to build them new; PJM capacity clears at its cap. The cheapest path to a contracted megawatt is almost never greenfield construction — it is buying, restarting, or re-leasing capacity that already exists and already touches the grid.

Land and water are the gating inputs, not the value. Powered Texas dirt spans ~$8,000/acre (raw, pre-boom) to $480,000/acre (to capture an energized site), and the constraint increasingly binds on water — benchmarked here at $460/AF (Vista Ridge raw) and in barrels per day — rather than on acreage.
