## 1. The scarce asset in Permian gas isn't the molecule or the acreage — it's a 2027–28 turbine slot next to load

**Consensus:** negative Waha is a takeaway problem that 2H26–2027 pipelines fix; data centers need power; gas is the answer.

**The read:** three sourced facts only make sense together. (a) The Waha forward
curve prices *negative* gas into 2028 — Summer-2027 strip −$1.98, Winter '27-'28
−$2.11 — i.e. the market does **not** believe the pipeline buildout resolves the
glut. (b) GE Vernova has **~10 GW of turbine capacity left across 2029–2030
combined**, with new bids pricing 10–20 pts higher $/kW and holding; Siemens sold
out through FY2028. (c) Diamondback said it's "on the cusp" of monetizing gas
into a power/data-center project worked for ~a year. Put together: stranded,
sub-zero associated gas is abundant and worthless at the wellhead; load is
desperate; the only thing standing between the two is a *machine you cannot buy
until 2029*. The binding constraint is the turbine, and whoever already holds
2027–28 slots controls the spread between −$2 gas and $80+/MWh power.

**Trade / tell:** the highest-return Permian gas position is not the molecule —
it's interconnection-adjacent land plus a turbine reservation. Watch which E&Ps
quietly disclose Slot Reservation Agreements or behind-the-meter gen JVs (FANG
flagged one); that disclosure, not the drillbit, is the alpha.

> Evidence: Aegis Hedging (forward Waha), May 2026; GE Vernova Q1 transcript /
> Utility Dive, Apr 2026; Diamondback Q1 transcript (Insider Monkey), May 2026.

## 2. Fuel cells, not reactors, fill the 2026–2029 power gap — and institutional capital just said so

**Consensus:** the "nuclear renaissance" powers the AI buildout.

**The read:** every nuclear path is dated past the gap. Oklo's ~15 GW book and
Kairos-Google-TVA don't deliver until ~2030; the restarts are stuck on
*paperwork*, not hardware (Palisades literally "cannot find the CMTR data" for
three welds; Crane earns no full PJM capacity credit until 2027). Turbines are
sold out to 2028–29. The only thing that ships into the 2026–2029 hole at scale
is on-site generation — and the **Bloom/Oracle 2.8 GW SOFC master agreement (1.2
GW already underway) plus Brookfield's up-to-$5B financing commitment** just
validated solid-oxide fuel cells as a financeable asset class. That is the quiet
winner the nuclear headlines are crowding out.

**Trade / tell:** the bridge-power equity story migrates to SOFC and reciprocating
engines for the next 36 months. Watch for the second and third hyperscaler SOFC
master agreement and the first SOFC ABS/project-finance print — that's when the
market reprices it from "science project" to infrastructure.

> Evidence: Oklo/Kairos (Wikipedia); Palisades / Industrial Info, Feb 2026;
> Bloom-Oracle IR + DCD/Brookfield, Apr 2026.

## 3. The Meta/Blue Owl 16-year residual-value guarantee — not the $27B — is the structural risk nobody's pricing

**Consensus:** Hyperion is a landmark $29.5B financing; the template for the sector.

**The read:** the headline size is noise. The signal is *how* an A+ rating
attached to a vehicle with a thin ~8–10% equity cushion: it required a **16-year
Meta residual-value guarantee** absorbing tech-obsolescence risk. So the
hyperscaler moved the asset off balance sheet but *kept the economic risk* via a
contingent guarantee — synthetic leverage that the rating agencies blessed.
GPUs depreciate over 3–5 years; the RVG runs 16. As the whole field copies the
"template" (S&P/MS's word), the sector accumulates a stack of off-balance-sheet,
long-dated obsolescence guarantees that no consolidated balance sheet shows. If
GPU useful life compresses, these RVGs are the contingent liability that bites
all at once.

**Trade / tell:** read the *guarantee structure*, not the deal size, in the next
hyperscaler SPV. The tell is whether RVG tenor keeps stretching while equity
cushions thin — that's the sector quietly levering up where credit analysts
aren't looking.

> Evidence: Global Data Center Hub (Hyperion structure), 2026.

## 4. Two unrelated regulatory footnotes hand a specific, dull supply chain the win

**Consensus:** OBBBA/FEOC and the tariffs are a generalized headwind for clean energy.

**The read:** cross two provisions nobody reads together. (a) The FEOC
material-assistance calc **excludes iron and steel** — a carve-out for
steel-heavy balance-of-system. (b) Section 232 **caps transformers/switchgear at
15%** through 2027 with a 15%-metal-content de minimis. Net: domestic
steel-intensive BOS (racking, foundations, transmission structures) is
structurally advantaged, while the genuinely binding scarcity across every
section of this wire — solar, storage, gas, nuclear, data centers — is the same
unglamorous box: the transformer. The edge isn't in modules, cells, or even
turbines; it's in the BOS/transformer supply chain that the policy regime just
quietly protected.

**Trade / tell:** transformer and switchgear lead times are the real load-growth
governor. Watch domestic transformer order books and BOS margins — that's where
the policy asymmetry cashes out, not in the panel.

> Evidence: Nixon Peabody (FEOC MACR), Mar 2026; Green Worldwide (§232), 2026.

## 5. The ERCOT 2028–29 trade is sitting in plain sight — an IPP just told you which side it's on

**Consensus:** ERCOT's 233 GW interconnection queue is mostly phantom load; even
ERCOT's own VP says "we don't expect all of those will materialize."

**The read:** that skepticism is *in the forward curve* — Constellation says the
ERCOT 2028–29 strip "appears like something that's only expecting 10,000–15,000
MW" of load growth. And Constellation, with a real book, is positioned the other
way: calling ERCOT "undervalued" for 2028–29 while staying "well hedged" near
term. So you have the market believing ERCOT's official phantom-load skepticism,
and one of the most sophisticated IPPs publicly fading it. That gap — curve vs.
realized 2028 load — is the single cleanest ERCOT-specific position, and SB6
(which turns large loads into mandatory curtailable resources) is the mechanism
that decides who's right by making "firm" load the scarce, premium product.

**Trade / tell:** the spread between the ERCOT 2028–29 forward and realized
interconnections is the position. The leading indicator is SB6 curtailment-docket
adoption and how much queue converts to *firm* (vs. interruptible) — firm load is
what actually moves the curve.

> Evidence: Constellation Q1 transcript (Investing.com), May 2026; Utility Dive
> (ERCOT queue), 2026; Perkins Coie (SB6), Mar 2026.

## 6. Brownfield gas is a call option on the turbine shortage — and it's still trading at intrinsic value

**Consensus:** brownfield PJM gas at $743–1,327/kW is a cheap value play vs.
$2,000+/kW greenfield.

**The read:** the discount exists *because* you can't get a turbine to build
greenfield until 2029–30. So an existing, interconnected gas plant isn't just
cheap capacity — it's a call option on the turbine shortage, and the option is
being sold near intrinsic value. As lead times extend and $/kW on new bids keeps
rising (GE Vernova +10–20 pts and climbing), brownfield should converge *up*
toward replacement cost. The Talen/ECP ($1,327/kW, ~6.6x 2027E EBITDA),
Vistra/Lotus ($743/kW), and NRG/LS Power ($12B) rollups are buying this option;
the sellers are handing over optionality for a multiple.

**Trade / tell:** the recognition signal is the next PJM/ERCOT brownfield gas deal
printing above ~$1,500/kW. That's the moment the arb is acknowledged and the
discount window closes.

> Evidence: Talen IR, 2026; NGI (Vistra/Lotus), 2026.

## 7. The death of green hydrogen quietly created a coherent Permian monetization stack

**Consensus:** OBBBA gutting 45V is bad for hydrogen, full stop.

**The read:** 45V (green H2) is dead; **45Q (carbon capture) survived at
$85/ton**, which doesn't kill hydrogen — it relocates it to where there's CO2
storage and cheap gas: the Gulf Coast and Permian. Blue hydrogen/ammonia now
*beats* green on policy, not just cost. Tie it back to item 1: negative-Waha gas
+ 45Q sequestration + blue-ammonia export is a single coherent thesis that turns
the stranded-gas problem into a monetization path. CF Industries' ~$100M/yr 45Q
free-cash stream off Donaldsonville is the live comp that proves the cash flow is
real, not a tax abstraction.

**Trade / tell:** watch Gulf Coast/Permian blue-ammonia FIDs and 45Q
transfer-credit pricing. The connective insight — stranded gas finds a home in
blue molecules — is the differentiated way to be long Permian gas without being
long the Waha price.

> Evidence: ING Think (45V/blue H2), 2026; IRS Notice 2026-01 + Beancount.io
> (45Q), 2026.

## 8. The "datacenter-in-space" filing is a real tell — about how unsolvable terrestrial siting looks to the people closest to demand

**Consensus:** orbital data centers are sci-fi; ignore.

**The read:** the point isn't feasibility. It's that SpaceX filed for **100 GW of
orbital compute (≈20% of US electricity)** three days before the xAI merger
closed, and Meta reserved up to 1 GW of space-based solar with first transmission
not until 2028. When the parties closest to compute demand are spending real
filing/reservation capital on exotic off-planet power, that is a revealed
preference about how constrained they believe *terrestrial* power-adjacent land
is. For a land/resource investor that's the signal: the scarce, bid-up asset is
the terrestrial parcel with fiber + water + gas + interconnection, and the smart
money is hedging into orbit precisely because the ground solution is so tight.

**Trade / tell:** treat orbital-compute and space-solar announcements as a
sentiment gauge on terrestrial siting scarcity, inversely. Every serious orbital
filing is a vote that prime power-adjacent land gets *more* valuable, not less.

> Evidence: SpaceNews (SpaceX FCC filing), Jan 2026; TechCrunch (Meta/Overview),
> Apr 2026.
