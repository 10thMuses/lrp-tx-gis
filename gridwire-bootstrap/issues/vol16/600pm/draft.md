## II. Lead: The First Hiking Cycle the AI Credit Stack Has Ever Seen

<div class="lead" markdown="1">

The print is in, and it is a split screen. Headline CPI rose 0.5% in May, putting the annual rate at 4.2%, the fastest since April 2023 and exactly in line with consensus. Core rose 0.2% on the month, below the 0.3% estimate, with the annual core rate at 2.9%, the highest since September 2025 but also in line. Energy jumped 3.9% in the month, is up 23.5% over twelve months, and accounted for more than 60% of the monthly all-items increase. Core commodities fell 0.1%, indicating muted tariff pass-through.^[3,11] The NSA index printed 335.123. Treasuries took it flat; equity futures pared losses on the release before the Iran tape took over (see §I, §XVI).

</div>

The honest read cuts both ways. The headline leg of the thesis is confirmed: the Iran-war energy shock is now compounding in the official data, two consecutive hot headline months, and the FOMC meets June 17 staring at a 4-handle. The hiking leg is tested but intact: the soft 0.2% monthly core is the strongest single data point the doves have received since the war began, and the market read it as relief that the energy shock has not yet spilled into broad prices. CME odds of a 2026 hike, north of 70% after Friday's jobs report, are the number to watch into the Fed meeting; a hold next week with hawkish language remains the base case.^[4] The structural exposure is unchanged either way: every delayed-draw term loan in the AI complex floats over SOFR, struck in a market that assumed the next move was down. The long end already made a 2007 high in May on the inflation leg alone. The hiking leg is optionality against the same positions, not the requirement.

## IV. Applied Digital Forensics: The Rating Is Now a Transferable Asset

Four APLD events in five days form one structure, and the June 5 CoreWeave MOU is the part the market read past. The assignment of the Building 3 lease is conditioned not on payment, not on completion, but on a CoreWeave subsidiary achieving an investment grade rating.^[13] Read against the financing stack it sits inside (Macquarie preferred at 12.75% PIK above, 6.750% secured notes at 98 beside, a SOFR+225 revolver on non-project collateral), the mechanic is explicit: tenant credit quality is being engineered at the subsidiary level so the landlord's project debt can re-rate. CoreWeave already terminated one APLD-adjacent arrangement in March when underlying tenant credit failed; this MOU is the corrected architecture.

::: angle
The DDTL 4.0 lesson has propagated: the rating, not the asset, is the negotiated commodity in AI infrastructure credit. Counterparties now structure SPVs to receive a rating the way they once structured them to receive collateral. Efficient while ratings hold, pro-cyclical when they do not, because every instrument keyed to the same rating event re-prices together. The recovery floor under all of it remains the land, power, and water at the project site, which carry no rating and need none.

**Falsification (status):** half the test resolved today. ELN-04 priced at 7.000% at par, inside the stated 7.5% threshold: the market is still paying for the structure, not charging for the asset. The 25 bps of widening versus March's 6.750% issue matches the rate backdrop, not a credit re-rating. The remaining test is the Building 3 assignment closing without the subsidiary's IG rating. Note the same-day equity read: APLD stock fell 5.7% on the pricing. Credit pays for the structure; equity pays for the leverage. That divergence is §V's CoreWeave pattern, one tier down the stack.
:::

## V. CoreWeave: The Equity Layer Is Selling What the Credit Layer Is Buying

Bloomberg, June 9: CoreWeave executives have sold more than $2.3B of stock since the March 2025 IPO, the bulk by co-founders Intrator, McBee, and Venturo; Intrator sold 307,692 shares for $37.7M on June 2 alone; Magnetar holds 9.7%.^[9,22] The same fourteen months produced the DDTL ladder ending in the $8.5B A3/A(low) facility at SOFR+2.25%. Q1 prints were strong on their own terms: revenue $2.1B (+112%), backlog $99.4B, more than 1 GW of active power; GAAP net loss widened to $740M.^[23] Russell 3000 inclusion lands June 26, importing index-mandated buyers.^[24]

::: angle
Lenders re-rated the company to investment grade in the same window its founders liquidated $2.3B of equity. Both sides can be right simultaneously only if the durable value sits below the equity claim: in the contracts, the sites, and the power, which is where the collateral agent's lien actually attaches. June 26 then hands the exit liquidity to index funds that buy by mandate, the same rating-not-fundamentals buyer base as the credit.

**Falsification:** sustained founder buying, or a clean DSCR test at first measurement in 2027 alongside spread compression through SOFR+200.
:::

## VI. Power and Grid: Batch Zero Is SB6's Enforcement Mechanism

The ERCOT board approved the Batch Zero framework June 1-2; it goes to the PUCT for final approval on ==June 18==.^[10,25] The queue it will sort now stands at 438 GW against a record system peak of roughly 85 GW, with ~200 data centers seeking connection in 2026 alone.^[10] Batch Zero replaces serial study with batch study and, critically, allocates available transmission capacity to studied and committed large loads. Combined with the Docket 58481 regime ($50,000/MW non-refundable fee, demonstrated site control, security forfeiture on withdrawal), the batch is a forced sort: phantom load dies in the study, controlled land survives it. Cipher's 1,000 MW Colchis project, with an executed AEP direct-connect agreement, states it expects inclusion in Batch Zero; that is what the survivor profile looks like.^[26]

## XII. Policy: A Court Just Restored the 5% Safe Harbor With 24 Days Left on the Clock

On June 6 the US District Court for DC, in Oregon Environmental Council v. IRS (No. 25-4400, CKK), vacated IRS Notice 2025-42 in full as arbitrary and capricious and remanded to the agency.^[29,30] The notice had eliminated the 5% safe harbor and made the physical-work test the only path for wind and large solar to establish beginning of construction ahead of the OBBBA's July 4, 2026 deadline. Projects that begin construction by July 4 keep the four-year continuity runway (in service by end-2030); projects that miss it must be in service by December 31, 2027.^[30,31] The decision stands unless stayed or reversed; the IRS may seek emergency relief.

::: angle
With 24 days to the statutory cliff, the vacatur converts a physical-work race into a procurement race: 5% safe harbor means qualifying spend, and qualifying spend means panels, trackers, transformers, and inverters bought before July 4. This is a compressed, deadline-driven equipment pull-forward landing on the §VII supply chain, where more than half of 2026's data centers are already delayed on electrical gear. The cliff was supposed to slow the buildout. For the next 24 days it accelerates demand on the exact components the AI buildout is short.

**Falsification:** an emergency stay or appellate reversal before July 4.

The same month, the PUCT decides the first 765 kV line of a network whose western terminus is the Solstice substation outside Fort Stockton. Three administrative acts, one direction: pre-held physical position in Pecos and Reeves counties is being converted into exclusive, transferable, rate-base-backed rights. Capital that shows up June 19 buys from whoever filed by June 18.

**Falsification:** the PUCT materially delays or rejects Batch Zero or the 765 kV CCN, or the Reeves GCD extends the HUP deadline past September 1, each of which defers (not destroys) the rights-formation event.
:::

## XVIII. Conclusions, Angled

**1.** The AI credit stack faces its first hiking cycle. Every SOFR-plus instrument was modeled in a cutting regime. This morning's CPI is the catalyst, not the cause.

**2.** The rating has become a transferable, negotiated asset (APLD/CoreWeave MOU). Peak financialization of the claim layer; it concentrates rather than distributes risk.

**3.** Founders sold $2.3B of the equity the credit market re-rated to A3. The capital structure is telling you where the value migrated: down, toward the contracts and the sites.

**4.** Four seniority systems closed or are closing at once: ERCOT transmission (Batch Zero), turbine slots (GEV 2029-2030), electrical equipment (Eaton's eleven-year backlog), and nuclear offtake (the $68.90-to-$102/MWh PPA tape). In each, money no longer buys position; only prior position does.

**5.** The June 6 vacatur turns the OBBBA cliff into a 24-day equipment procurement sprint landing on a supply chain that has already delayed half of 2026's data centers. The OBBBA itself is a sort, not a kill: storage, geothermal, and nuclear keep full credit into the 2030s, and capital is migrating accordingly.

**6.** Diamondback's green light is a gas-supply announcement wearing an oil headline: crude-priced rigs extend the negative-Waha regime that underwrites every behind-the-meter Permian power project.

**7.** Midstream is writing the longest, hardest paper in the complex: 2041 ethane contracts and 20-year laterals at sub-6x build multiples, while the AI claim layer leases at n/d tenors to engineered-credit SPVs. The valuation gap between those two layers is the standing mispricing.

**8.** June 18: transmission rights and senior water rights crystallize into property on the same Texas calendar day, with the first 765 kV order due the same month, terminating at Solstice. Pre-held physical position in Pecos and Reeves is the only asset all three processes reward.
