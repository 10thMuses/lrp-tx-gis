## II. Lead: De-Escalation Repriced the Macro. The Claim Layer Repriced Itself.

<div class="lead" markdown="1">

Two prints in the window, and they point in opposite directions. The macro one: Trump called off Thursday-night strikes, announced a 60-day ceasefire that reopens the Strait of Hormuz, and crude gave back 4% while the Dow put up its best day of the quarter, +930.^[1,4,5] The micro one arrived the same twenty-four hours and got none of the relief: Oracle guided fiscal-2027 capex to as much as $95B against negative $23.7B free cash flow and lost 9.4% of its equity on a +1.86% index day, and CoreWeave paid 9.625% for unsecured dollar paper while its own secured facility sits at SOFR+2.25.^[7,8,9] The war premium is leaving the barrel. It is not leaving the AI credit stack, because it was never the war holding that premium up.

</div>

The rates read is unchanged and now cleaner: the June 17 FOMC is a ~96.5% hold, no 2026 cuts are priced anywhere on the curve, and the probability of a hike within a year was still rising on June 11 even as the long end rallied to ~4.95-5.01%.^[2,3] The energy shock that drove two hot headline CPI months is now reversing at the commodity level, which deflates the inflation leg going forward; the 23.5% of trailing energy inflation already in the CPI base does not reverse with it. That asymmetry, stated in Vol 16 §XVI, gets its first live test over the next two CPI cycles. The structural exposure is also unchanged: every SOFR-plus instrument in the AI complex was struck assuming the next move was down, and the market has now removed that assumption without replacing it.

::: angle
The ceasefire is the best thing that has happened to the AI credit stack's funding math this quarter, and the stack repriced wider anyway. On the single best risk day since the war began, the marginal cost of unsecured AI infrastructure debt printed 9.625% and the largest cloud backlog ever recorded traded down 9%. When the claim layer cannot rally on a +930 Dow, the discount is not geopolitical. It is the funding gap.

**Falsification (status after the window):** the Vol 16 stated condition was core at or below 2.7% with hike odds below 40%; core printed 2.9%, not triggered, and the hold-with-hawkish-language base case is now ~96.5% priced. New named risk: a signed, executed Hormuz reopening deflates the energy leg faster than the hiking leg matures; if July CPI energy turns negative M/M and 2026 hike odds collapse below 40%, the lead thesis loses its catalyst and reverts to the long-end leg alone.
:::

## III. Oracle Forensics: The Largest Claim Ever Printed Came With a Funding Test

Oracle's Q4 FY26 print (June 10, after the bell) is the claim layer in one filing. Record revenue: $19.2B in the quarter, +21%; cloud $9.9B, +47%; infrastructure $5.8B, +93%. Record backlog: remaining performance obligations of $638B, up 363% Y/Y and up $85B sequentially.^[7] The funding side of the same filing: Q4 capex $15.9B, full-year $55.7B against $50B guided; fiscal-2027 capex guided up to $95B; free cash flow negative $23.7B for the year; return on invested capital down from 14.8% to 10.7%.^[8] The market read the second list: ORCL opened down 11.1% and closed down 9.4% on a day the Dow rose 930 points. A shareholder litigation probe into whether management downplayed the capex trajectory opened within twenty-four hours.^[8]

The detail that matters for the physical-layer thesis: Oracle disclosed that most of the Q3 and Q4 RPO additions were large-scale AI contracts in which the customer either prepaid Oracle for the GPUs or bought and supplied the GPUs themselves, with those prepaid and customer-supplied hardware portions totaling $75B.^[7]

::: angle
Customers are now paying the cloud vendor in hardware. A $75B slice of the most celebrated backlog number ever printed is not future compute demand in the ordinary sense; it is counterparties pre-positioning physical GPUs inside someone else's funding structure because neither side trusts the other to finance delivery later. That is the turbine-slot logic arriving inside the hyperscaler P&L: the scarce thing is funded, delivered hardware in an energized shell, and contracts are being written to secure it in kind. Meanwhile the $638B claim must be served by capex Oracle does not generate internally; at negative $23.7B FCF, every dollar of that buildout is somebody's new debt in a no-cut rate regime.

**Falsification:** Oracle funds the FY27 program without material net new issuance, or ROIC inflects upward while the backlog converts, either of which would prove the claim layer self-financing at current rates.
:::

## IV. CoreWeave: The Unsecured Print Marks the Stack

CoreWeave announced and priced a $3.5B two-tranche senior unsecured offering in the window: $1.25B at 9.625% and EUR 2B at 8.500%, both due July 15, 2032, guaranteed on a senior unsecured basis by certain wholly-owned subsidiaries, institutional and non-US buyers only, proceeds to general corporate purposes including repayment of outstanding debt, closing expected June 18.^[9] The equity closed flat (+0.14% at $95.74) on the strongest index day of the quarter and sits more than 20% below its June 1 peak.^[10]

The print completes the capital-structure picture Vol 16 §IV-V assembled. Secured, ratings-engineered paper at the project level: CRWV DDTL 4.0 at SOFR+2.25 (~5.9% indicative), APLD ComputeCo 3 at 7.000%. The same complex unsecured at the parent: 9.625%. The 370 bps between CoreWeave's own secured and unsecured cost is the market's stated price for everything above the collateral: the corporate claim, the residual, the equity cushion the founders have been selling.

## VI. Crude: The War Premium Exits the Barrel

*Kept strictly separate from the gas analysis below.* The 60-day ceasefire announced June 11 carries the physical terms that matter: the Strait of Hormuz opens with no tolls, Iran clears its own mines within 30 days, the US lifts the port blockade and issues sanctions waivers restoring Iranian export flow, and the enriched-uranium stockpile is the first negotiation item inside the window.^[5] WTI closed $87.71, fell to $86.51 in extended trade, and is lower again premarket; the de-escalation has taken roughly $8 off the barrel in two sessions.^[4,13] Physical normalization lags the headline: mines must actually clear, idled fields restart, and damaged facilities repair before flows normalize, and a June reopening was already viewed as unlikely by tanker analysts.^[6] The supply stack behind the reopening is unchanged from Vol 16: OPEC+ adding 188,000 b/d in July, record US exports, Chinese imports at 8-year lows, and the crude-side shale ramp arriving into it. A signed deal converts Fitch's end-July reopening model from forecast to schedule, with Q4 oversupply as the printed risk case.

## VII. Natural Gas: The Surplus Builds While Crude De-Escalates

The June 11 storage report printed a 108 Bcf injection for the week ending June 5 against a 99 Bcf consensus and a 110 Bcf year-ago build, widening the surplus to the five-year average from 138 to 151 Bcf; futures sold the print.^[14] The June 9 STEO did the structural work: May Henry Hub averaged $2.94, EIA expects ~$3.34 for the second half of 2026, and the 2027 forecast was cut by $1.13 versus January, to $3.46, explicitly on the storage overhang.^[15] Waha marks are unchanged in the window; the latest published remain May 22 (Summer strip -$4.69, cash near -$3.00).^[16] The gas-side thesis is unchanged and the week strengthened it: crude-priced rigs keep adding associated gas into a hub already in surplus, and Baker Hughes prints the rig count at 1:00 PM ET today against Enverus's ~240.

## X. Conclusions, Angled

**1.** The war premium is leaving crude and was never what held up the AI credit stack's pricing. On the best risk day of the quarter, the claim layer's marginal unsecured cost printed 9.625% and the largest backlog ever recorded lost 9% of its equity value.

**2.** Oracle disclosed $75B of its $638B RPO as prepaid or customer-supplied GPUs. Counterparties are securing physical hardware in kind inside other companies' funding structures. That is the physical-layer thesis written into an issuer disclosure.

**3.** CoreWeave's 370 bps secured-to-unsecured gap inside one capital structure is the printed price of the claim above the collateral. Vol 16 argued the durable value sits at the project layer; the unsecured market just agreed.

**4.** The hiking-cycle lead thesis survives the window but acquires a named decay path: a signed Hormuz reopening deflates the energy leg while the hold-with-hawkish-language base case (~96.5%) plays out. The long-end leg, a ~5% 30-year, does not depend on it.

**5.** Correction discipline: Vol 16 carried NextEra/Dominion as exploratory; it was a definitive $66.8B all-stock merger signed May 18. The correction strengthens the underlying read: the largest utility combination on record is a purchase of load-pocket position and a ~130 GW interconnection pipeline.

**6.** June 18 concentrates the Texas rights-formation calendar and the AI complex's funding calendar into one day. Pre-held physical position in Pecos and Reeves still costs the same; the paper claims against it got more expensive this week.
