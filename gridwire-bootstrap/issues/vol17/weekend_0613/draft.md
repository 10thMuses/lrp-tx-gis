## Lead: The Energy Leg Started to Give. The Funding Leg Did Not.

<div class="lead" markdown="1">

The week ended with the macro and the micro pointing in opposite directions, and the divergence is the entire story. On the macro side, the US and Iran finalized the text of a 60-day ceasefire that reopens the Strait of Hormuz; crude gave back roughly $8 in two sessions, the Dow rose 930 points on June 11 and another 354 on June 12, and the long end of the Treasury curve eased.<sup>1,2,5</sup> On the micro side, the same forty-eight hours produced a $95B fiscal-2027 capex guide from Oracle against negative $23.7B free cash flow, a 9.4% single-day decline in the most valuable backlog ever recorded, and a 9.625% unsecured coupon from the investment-grade-rated leader of the neocloud complex.<sup>7,8,9</sup>

</div>

The conventional read is that de-escalation is risk-on and therefore good for everything financed. The order of causation is the opposite. The war premium that drove two consecutive hot headline CPI prints is now leaving the barrel, which deflates the inflation leg going forward; the 23.5% of trailing energy inflation already embedded in the CPI base does not reverse with the spot price. The Fed meets June 17 priced at a roughly 96.5% hold, with no 2026 cuts anywhere on the curve and the probability of a hike within a year still rising.<sup>3</sup> Every delayed-draw term loan and powered-shell project financing in the AI complex was struck assuming the next move was down. The ceasefire removes the supply shock that would have forced a hike; it does not restore the cut the structures were modeled against.

::: box
**Practitioner read.** The cleanest single fact of the week is that the AI credit stack repriced wider on the best macro tape of the quarter. CoreWeave's marginal unsecured cost printed 9.625% and Oracle's $638B backlog lost 9% of its equity value on a day the Dow rose 930 points. When the claim layer cannot rally on de-escalation, the discount is not geopolitical and it is not a demand question. It is the gap between what has been contracted and what can be financed, built, and powered on the contracted timeline. The financed layer (debt, equity, contracts) moved this week. The physical layer (land, water, stranded gas, transmission, turbine slots) did not, and it became relatively more valuable as everything that must be financed to compete for it got more expensive.
:::

## II. The Bond Move and the AI Financing Stack

The 30-year near 5% and the 10-year near 4.4% are not abstract macro prints. They are the benchmark off which every layer of the AI-infrastructure capital stack is priced, and the ceasefire eased them at the margin without changing the regime.

<div class="tbl t-2col" markdown="1">

| Stack layer | Benchmark sensitivity and what a ~5% long end does |
|---|---|
| Hyperscaler-backed IG DDTLs | Spread over Treasuries; reprices the cheapest tier of the stack up first as the base rate holds high. |
| Single-asset data-center SPV bonds | OAS over the BB index, which itself moves with Treasuries; base rate plus spread widening compound. |
| Neocloud HY DDTLs (CoreWeave-style) | SOFR plus spread; refinance math tightens against 4-5 year depreciating GPU collateral on 7-year contracts. |
| Powered-shell project finance | Long-dated, levered to the 30-year; the most directly hit, because these are 15-30 year cash-flow assets. |
| Equity tranche (neocloud common) | Discount rate equals risk-free plus premium; multiple compression as the risk-free anchor holds near a 2007 high. |

</div>

The ceasefire introduces the first potential circuit-breaker in the reflexive loop this briefing has tracked since the daily cuts: energy shock drives the inflation prints, the prints drive the long-end repricing, the long end reprices the entire AI financing stack, and the financing stress does nothing to relieve the energy shock that started it. A signed Hormuz reopening attacks the first link. It does not touch the second branch, financial repression, in which the Treasury caps the long end via shortened issuance, buybacks, and jawboned central-bank purchases, easing nominal financing cost while debasing the real return on the long-dated, levered cash flows that powered-shell projects depend on.

::: box
**Composite implication.** Both branches of the rate path are negative for the financed layer and neutral-to-positive for the physical layer. Higher-for-longer at the long end raises the cost of capital directly; financial repression debases the real return on the levered claim. A controlled acre, an adjudicated acre-foot, and a 2029 turbine slot are real assets that carry a higher cost of capital better than a levered claim does, and they are the classic hedge against a debased curve. The week's de-escalation eases the first branch and pushes the system toward the second; neither branch is the one on which the financed claim outperforms the unlevered physical input.
:::

**CoreWeave (CRWV).** The DSC covenant on $8.5B of GPU-collateralized debt, where the collateral depreciates over 4-5 years against 7-plus-year contracts, is the single most important sentence in the AI-infrastructure credit complex, and a ~5% long end just made the first test materially harder to clear. The May 18 DDTL 5.0 added the first publicly syndicated, secondary-tradable HPC term loan, formalizing GPU-backed paper as an asset class at SOFR+450, 225 bps wide of the IG-rated DDTL 4.0. The June 11 unsecured print sits 370 bps wider still.

**Nebius (NBIS).** Cleanest balance sheet of the three. Q1: revenue $399.0M (+684% YoY); GAAP net income $621.2M driven by a $780.6M equity revaluation, not operations; adjusted net loss $100.3M; ~$4.34B convertible notes outstanding.<sup>25</sup> Broke ground May 12 on a gigawatt-scale AI factory in Independence, Missouri (~400 acres, ~$650M of tax payments over 20 years, 1,200 construction and 130 permanent jobs); Pennsylvania owned AI factory up to 1.2 GW; Nvidia $2B strategic investment supports a >5 GW deployment target by 2030. Funds expansion via share issuance: dilution is the cost, not covenant risk.

**IREN (IREN).** Lowest leverage. The Nvidia agreement grants the right to invest up to $2.1B; in exchange IREN deploys up to 5 GW of Nvidia DSX-branded infrastructure; Microsoft is 57% of the 2026 AI cloud ARR target.

::: box
**Practitioner read.** The three-name comparison resolves cleanly after the week's bond move: Nebius (equity-funded) and IREN (low-leverage) are structurally more survivable in a higher-for-longer environment than CoreWeave at 7.49x debt-to-equity with a DSC covenant on depreciating collateral. The relative-value separator inside the neocloud cohort is leverage, not growth rate. CoreWeave's unsecured print did not change its growth; it marked its leverage.
:::

## V. The Hyperscaler Deals and Capex: Footnote-Level

**Oracle (the funding test).** Q4 FY26 (June 10, after the close): revenue $19.2B (+21%), cloud $9.9B (+47%), infrastructure $5.8B (+93%); remaining performance obligations of $638B, up 363% year-over-year and up $85B sequentially.<sup>7</sup> The funding side of the same filing: Q4 capex $15.9B, full-year $55.7B against $50B guided; fiscal-2027 capex guided to as much as $95B; free cash flow negative $23.7B for the year; return on invested capital down from 14.8% to 10.7%.<sup>8</sup> The market read the second list: the stock opened down 11.1%, closed down 9.4% on a day the Dow rose 930 points, and a shareholder-litigation probe into the capex disclosure opened within twenty-four hours. The structural detail: most of the Q3 and Q4 RPO additions were large AI contracts in which the customer either prepaid for the GPUs or bought and supplied them, with those hardware portions totaling $75B.<sup>7</sup>

::: box
**Angle.** Customers are paying the cloud vendor in hardware. A $75B slice of the most celebrated backlog number ever printed is not future compute demand in the ordinary sense; it is counterparties pre-positioning physical GPUs inside someone else's funding structure because neither side trusts delivery to be financeable later. That is turbine-slot logic arriving inside the hyperscaler P&L: the scarce input is funded, delivered hardware in an energized shell, and contracts are now written to secure it in kind. The $638B claim must be served by capex Oracle does not generate internally; at negative $23.7B free cash flow, every dollar of the buildout is new debt issued into a no-cut rate regime.
:::

## VIII. Power Generation Deals: Chevron, Stargate, and the Shadow Grid

**Chevron / Engine No. 1 / Microsoft.** Microsoft holds exclusivity on the 2.5 GW (expandable to 5 GW) Permian behind-the-meter project, ~$7B, with seven GE Vernova turbines on order (three-year lead, 2029 delivery) and first power targeted late 2027. Definitive terms remain n/d.

**Stargate.** Now an umbrella term, not first-party infrastructure: eight sites announced; 600 MW Abilene expansion; a 5 GW US-UAE campus in Abu Dhabi (first 1 GW cluster G42-built, OpenAI/Oracle-operated, Nvidia GB300), part of UAE's repositioning as the sovereign-AI oil-export hybrid counter to Saudi Humain.

**The shadow grid.** Global Energy Monitor counts 1,000-plus GW of gas-fired power in development worldwide (+31% in a year); Texas has ~58 GW of gas in planning or construction, more than the next four states combined: Pacifico GW Ranch 7.65 GW (largest disclosed), Chevron Pecos 2.5-5 GW, Crusoe 4.5 GW for Stargate. All gas-anchored, all Texas, all dependent on the turbine bottleneck in Section VII.

::: box
**Practitioner read.** The PJM independent market monitor (Monitoring Analytics) found data centers directly caused a 75.5% wholesale-power increase in the largest US power region, from $77.78/MWh (Q1 2025) to $136.53/MWh (Q1 2026), and called the impact "very large and not reversible," recommending large loads negotiate directly with producers rather than mix demand into the capacity auction.<sup>28</sup> That is the East Coast precedent validating the off-grid, behind-the-meter, gas-anchored Texas model from the regulatory side. "Not reversible" converts a transient price spike into a structural cost step-change in the densest US data-center corridor, which raises the relative attractiveness of dedicated Permian generation that never touches a socialized auction. The June 2026 PJM base residual auction is a hard catalyst on the same clock as ERCOT Batch Zero and the June 17 FOMC.

::: box
**Composite implication.** The financial-repression endgame remains the single most important scenario for the AI-infrastructure financing stack, and the ceasefire moves the system toward it. Named, on-record practitioners (Daleep Singh on the "bond-vigilante trade," Jamie Dimon on a "bond crisis") have described 5%-plus long yields as fiscally unsustainable and a Treasury response (shortened issuance, buybacks, jawboned long-end purchases) as the likely terminal state. If the ceasefire deflates the oil leg, it relieves the inflation print that was forcing the hike, but it does not restore the cut the AI structures were modeled against, and it pushes policy toward capping the long end via repression, which debases the real return on exactly the long-dated, levered cash flows the powered-shell projects depend on.
:::

## XVI. Novel Angles and Second-Order Signals

**1. The energy leg is giving; the funding leg is not.** On the best macro tape of the quarter, unsecured AI-infrastructure debt printed 9.625% and the largest backlog ever recorded lost 9% of its equity value. The binding variable is cost of capital, not demand or technology.

**2. Customers are paying the cloud vendor in hardware.** $75B of Oracle's $638B RPO is prepaid or customer-supplied GPUs: counterparties securing physical hardware in kind inside other companies' funding structures.

**3. The secured-to-unsecured gap is now printed inside one issuer.** CoreWeave's 370 bps is the market's stated price for the corporate claim above the collateral.

**4. GPU paper is a formalized asset class.** DDTL 5.0 is the first publicly syndicated, secondary-tradable HPC term loan; the next deal's spread tells you whether IG balance sheets are absorbing it.

**5. Slot reservations are a tradable real option.** GE Vernova's 56 GW of slot reservations on top of 44 GW of firm backlog is a held call on 2029-2030 generation siting; watch for secondary slot transfers.

**6. "Not reversible" is the East Coast precedent.** PJM's monitor recommending direct producer contracts validates the off-grid Texas model from the regulatory side.

**7. The IPO window and the credit window are open in opposite directions.** SpaceX held +19% the same week CoreWeave paid 9.625% unsecured.
