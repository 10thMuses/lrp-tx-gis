Master content/insight package for the 2026-08 redesign. Every builder (deck or
document) draws facts, numbers, and headline/subheadline copy from this file —
do not invent figures not sourced here. Adapt tone/density/layout to your

## 0. Non-negotiable rules

1. **No hyperbole, no promotional adjectives.** This is a sales document that
   works through technical specificity, not superlatives. Never write "world-
   class," "unprecedented," "incredible," etc. Let the numbers carry it.
2. **Every heading gets a subheading that is a conclusion, not a label.**
   Wrong: "Regional Power Cluster." Right: "Regional Power Cluster — subheading:
   "32.7 GW of operating and queued capacity sits within 60 miles; Caramba North
   is inside the radius, not adjacent to it." The subheading states the
   so-what in one sentence, specific and numeric where possible.
3. **Longfellow / Project Horizon: infrastructure-first, no tenant-exit
   narrative.** Do not mention CoreWeave, its exit, or any tenant-transition
   detail. Frame Longfellow entirely around its site fundamentals and planned
   on-site power generation. If a status line is unavoidable, use neutral
   language ("phase-1 site work underway") — never dwell on tenancy.
4. **Distances are edge-to-edge, not centroid-to-centroid.** GW Ranch ≈ 15.5
   miles, Longfellow ≈ 19.3 miles from the Caramba North tract boundary (see
   §4). Do not use the older centroid figures (17.3 / 19.7 mi) anywhere.
5. **Cite the source class inline or in a footnote** for anything not already
   in the GIS data model (ERCOT queue growth figures, TCEQ permit language,
   news items). Public-data figures from the existing model don't need
   re-citation beyond what's already in the Appendix/source register.
6. **Density is the one thing that changes by format**, not accuracy: the
   full decks carry every stat in §2-§8; the two condensed documents carry
   the "keep" column only (marked in each section).

## 1. Positioning — the one idea, if there's only five minutes

Caramba North is not a speculative land bet — it's a **1,300-acre parcel
sitting inside an already-forming power and data-center corridor**: two
hyperscale-scale projects (7.65 GW and 2 GW) sit on the same north-south line
through the property at 15.5 and 19.3 miles, backed by a transmission,
water, and gas position that is already permitted, not proposed. The
region's own numbers — not the site's — do the selling: 32.7 GW of
operating + queued power capacity within 60 miles, in a state where the
interconnection backlog just got large enough (474 GW, 90% data centers) to
trigger a gubernatorial audit and a queue-processing pause. Caramba North is
positioned to benefit from the same infrastructure buildout without carrying
the exposure of being the marginal, unproven project in that queue — it
already has water and gas under contract-ready terms, not just an
application.

## 2. Core facts, by section

### 2.1 The Property
- 1,300 acres max contiguous, north side of I-10, Pecos County.
- ~5 miles from Fort Stockton (services, regional airport).
- No zoning ordinance — industrial/energy use as of right.
- Far West weather zone — ERCOT's highest-growth large-load pocket.
- **Subheading candidate:** "As-of-right industrial land inside the fastest-
  growing load pocket in ERCOT — not a rezoning story."

### 2.2 Transmission
- 15 miles from AEP/CPS Energy's Solstice Substation — western terminus of
  three PUCT-approved 765 kV Permian import paths (approved Apr 24, 2025,
  PBRP Docket No. 55718).
- Six local substations within 10 miles: Fort Stockton Plant (2.0 mi,
  138/69 kV), Airport (3.3 mi, 138 kV), Fort Stockton (5.4 mi, 69 kV), 16th
  Street (6.0 mi, 138/69 kV), Northern Natural (7.0 mi), Gomez (9.7 mi).
- 141 substation + 133 line upgrades tracked ERCOT-wide under TPIT (Transmission
  Planning Improvement Tool) — the queue of *planned* grid upgrades, not
  built yet; cite as pipeline context, not committed capacity.
- **Subheading candidate:** "Fifteen miles from the delivery point of all
  three approved 765 kV Permian import lines — the transmission decision is
  already made, upstream of this site."

### 2.3 Regional Power Cluster (Pecos County)
- Operating: 3,226 MW (Solar 2,178 MW/13 projects, Wind 542 MW/5, BESS 505
  MW/6, Gas 1 MW/1).
- ERCOT queue: 12,039 MW / 39 projects in Pecos County alone.
- Adjacent six counties (Reeves, Crane, Ward, Upton, Ector, Crockett):
  7,022 MW operating, 24,585 MW queued.
- Within 20 miles specifically: 13 queued projects, 3,973 MW.
- Nearest operating storage: St. Gall Energy Storage I (1.9 mi, 103 MW BESS).
- **Subheading candidate:** "12 GW already queued in this county alone —
  before counting the two hyperscale campuses profiled in §2.6."

### 2.4 Water
- 47,418 AF/yr (42.3 MGD) permitted on adjacent affiliated lands — roughly
  two-thirds of all Middle Pecos GCD industrial rights.
- Source: Edwards-Trinity (Plateau) aquifer; recharge held through the 1950s
  drought of record.
- **Subheading candidate:** "Two-thirds of the district's industrial water
  rights are already permitted to this position — the water conversation is
  closed, not open."

### 2.5 Natural Gas
- 20 miles to Waha hub.
- Indicative supply quote in hand: 200,000 MMBtu/day, 15-year term, Waha-
  index pricing; CIAC $15-25M; lead time 9-15 months (counterparty-supplied).

- **Subheading candidate:** "A signable 15-year gas quote at Waha basis — the

**GW Ranch (Amazon) — feature profile**
- 8,000-acre site, Pecos County, **≈15.5 miles** from Caramba North
  (edge-to-edge; TCEQ record: "~17 mi north of Fort Stockton on Highway 18").
- Amazon disclosed ownership Aug 2026 (previously Pacifico Energy Group,
  which remains power-plant developer/operator).
- 35 gas turbines, 7.65 GW TCEQ air permit (issued Jan/Feb 2026 — largest
  in the US), plus 1.8 GW battery storage, up to 750 MW solar.
- Three 189,000 sq ft data-center buildings (Gensler design, ~$300M each),
  targeted completion Dec 2026; ~$12B estimated total project investment.
- Status: **under construction** — 79.3% of the two profiled anchors'
  combined announced MW is already under construction, not merely proposed.
- Clarification to retain: the 7.65 GW figure is a TCEQ *generation* air
  permit, not an ERCOT interconnection queue position; Amazon has not
  disclosed an ERCOT filing and the project is off-grid initially. This sits
  inside the state-level audit context in §3.
- **Subheading candidate:** "The largest air permit issued in the US this
  year sits fifteen miles up the same highway corridor — under construction,
  not announced."

**Longfellow — feature profile (infrastructure-first; no tenant narrative)**
- 568-acre site, Pecos County, **≈19.3 miles** from Caramba North
  (edge-to-edge). Longfellow's own public materials describe the location as
  "more than 25 miles outside of Fort Stockton" — do not round this closer.
- On-site natural-gas generation planned: aero-derivative turbines with SCR
  and carbon-capture capability; closed-loop cooling on permitted
  non-potable groundwater.
- Originally announced Oct 2025 as a 2 GW, 8-phase (250 MW/phase) campus.
- Status language to use: "phase-1 site work underway; on-site generation
  build planned in phases." Do not state a specific current MW target if
  unclear — do not mention any tenant by name.
- No confirmed ERCOT queue position or TCEQ air-permit record found for this
  site as of Aug 2026 — state this plainly as a fact about permitting
  status, not as commentary on the project's viability.
- **Subheading candidate:** "A second phased gas-generation campus twenty
  miles south — the corridor's demand for on-site power isn't one project
  deep."

**Project-maturity framing (from the two anchors above):**
79.3% of combined announced capacity (GW Ranch) is under construction;
20.7% (Longfellow) is in planned/phase-1 status. Use this to make the point
that the regional pipeline is majority-built, not majority-speculative.

## 3. Macro context (use as framing, 1-2 uses per document, not throughout)

- ERCOT's large-load interconnection queue grew from 63 GW (end of 2024) to
  226 GW (Nov 2025) — nearly quadrupling in a year; ~77% of that load is
  data centers targeting 2030 interconnection (Latitude Media, Dec 3 2025).
- By Aug 2026 the statewide backlog reached ~474 GW of pending requests
  (~90% data-center-driven) — "more than five times Texas' record peak"
  demand per Gov. Abbott — prompting an Aug 3, 2026 directive to audit all
  ERCOT-queue data centers and a pause of the "Batch Zero" large-load review
  process pending that audit (Utility Dive).
- Frame: the region around Caramba North (32.7 GW within 60 mi) sits inside
  a state-level queue so large it triggered a regulatory pause — the demand
  signal is real enough to have created a policy problem, which is a
  different claim than "this area is growing."
- Source class: cite as "public reporting, Aug 2026" in condensed
  documents; full citation in the full-deck appendix/source register:
  - Latitude Media, "ERCOT's large load queue has nearly quadrupled in a
    single year" (Dec 3, 2025)
  - Utility Dive, "Facing an estimated 474 GW of interconnection requests,
    Texas hits pause on data centers" (Aug 2026)

## 6. Style briefs (full decks)

Every deck: LAYOUT_WIDE (13.333×7.5in), react-icons+sharp icon rendering
pattern from `scripts/build_caramba_om_pptx.js` is reusable infrastructure
— read that file for the icon/shadow/exhibitImage helpers before building.
Every slide heading must carry an insight subheading per Rule 2. Validate
with `/mnt/skills/public/pptx/scripts/office/validate.py`, then render to
images (`soffice --convert-to pdf` + `pdftoppm`) and visually inspect every
slide before calling it done.

**A. Institutional / Banker** (`Caramba-North-Deck-Institutional.pptx`)
Sharpen, don't just reuse, the existing navy/red/Calibri system: denser
stat tiles, more tables, add the ring-analysis table and gravity diagram as
a full slide. Subheadings sit directly under each title in a smaller serif
or italic treatment. Aim for ~16-18 slides — this is the "everything"
version for someone who will read all of it.

**B. Editorial / Narrative** (`Caramba-North-Deck-Editorial.pptx`)
Magazine layout: big pull-quote subheadings (the insight statements from
this brief, set large, often as the dominant visual element of the slide),
generous whitespace, fewer bullets, tables replaced with 2-3 called-out
numbers per slide set in large type. Photography-style full-bleed treatment
for the map exhibits. Aim for ~12-14 slides — same substance, told as a
sequence of findings rather than a spec sheet.

**C. Map-and-Infographic-led** (`Caramba-North-Deck-MapLed.pptx`)
Every slide is built map/diagram-first — the exhibit or gravity diagram
takes 60-70% of the slide, with callout leader-lines pointing to 3-5 key
numbers laid directly on or beside the visual, heading+subheading in a
compact band at top. Minimize prose paragraphs; convert body copy into
labeled callouts. Aim for ~12-14 slides.

**D. Minimalist Executive** (`Caramba-North-Deck-Minimalist.pptx`)
One idea per slide. Oversized type (headline ~40-54pt, subheading ~20-24pt
acting as the real content), at most one supporting stat row or one small
exhibit crop per slide, huge negative space. This is the version built to
be read in five minutes — cut the two-tables-per-slide + all named-project
line items into the ~10 numbers that matter most, aim for ~10-12 slides.

## 7. Style briefs (condensed documents, 5-6 pages, portrait, PDF + Word)

Both use the pdf and docx skills — read those SKILL.md files before
building. Portrait, single-column preferred; a 5-6 page ceiling means every
section from §2 is compressed to its 1-2 highest-value facts plus its
subheading — cut named line items, keep totals.

**Doc 1 — "Executive Brief"** (`Caramba-North-Brief-Executive.pdf/.docx`)
Reads like a one-page-per-topic memo: headline + insight subheading + 3-4
sentence paragraph + one supporting number or small exhibit crop per page.
Cover page carries the positioning statement from §1 verbatim (or tightened)
as the lead paragraph, plus the power-gravity diagram. This is the "CEO has
five minutes" version — no tables, prose only, numbers inline.

**Doc 2 — "Technical Snapshot"** (`Caramba-North-Brief-Technical.pdf/.docx`)
Denser, table/stat-grid driven: a facts table per section (property,
transmission, power, water/gas, pipeline, subsurface) with the subheading
as a header row above each table, minimal prose. Two-column layout where
portrait width allows. This is for the reader who wants the data-room
numbers, not the narrative — visually distinct from Doc 1 (grid-based vs.
prose-based) even though the content is the same facts.
